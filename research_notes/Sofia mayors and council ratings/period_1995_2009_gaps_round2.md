## 1. Property swaps ("заменки"), sales and in-kind contributions of municipal property 1998–2009

### Takeaway
No count, area or loss figure for council-approved swaps was found; the hard numbers that exist are for the municipal company Sofiyski imoti: about 350–400 properties by 2000, nearly 1,000 deals in 1998–2003, and a state financial-control audit that put the loss from its joint companies at "at least 33 million leva". The prosecutions largely failed: in the case brought in 2008 (13 preliminary sale contracts, about 2 million leva) the city court acquitted Toshko Dobrev and 13 co-defendants in 2012, the appellate court later terminated the case, and Dobrev has since won compensation from the prosecution.

### Cited Findings
Scale and finances of Sofiyski imoti
- [REPORTED, quoting the director] Early 2000: Dobrev said the company started "five years ago" with one secretary and 10 properties and now had 350–400. It was created so that its properties could serve as collateral for loans to the municipality. — [Capital, 7 Apr 2000 (archived copy read)](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2000/04/07/255184_sofiiskite_finansovi_potainosti/)
- [REPORTED] 1999 results: net profit 797,000 leva, dividend to the municipality 359,000 leva, on properties valued at 21 million leva "at understated prices". The company sponsored concerts, music videos and films (including events of the foundation of the prime minister's wife) and refused to disclose the amounts. — [Capital, 7 Apr 2000](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2000/04/07/255184_sofiiskite_finansovi_potainosti/)
- [REPORTED] Around 1999 the company became the main shareholder of PFC Slavia with 34%, paying by an in-kind contribution of the municipal land under the stadium in Ovcha kupel; other shareholders were people linked to the former SIC group. In 1997 it formed a joint company with partners linked to VIS-2. The Maria Luiza baths complex (book value 150,000 leva) was rented to a VIS-2-linked firm for USD 15,000 a year. — [Capital, 7 Apr 2000](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2000/04/07/255184_sofiiskite_finansovi_potainosti/)
- [REPORTED] 2003: the municipality spent 8.2 million leva on its municipal companies and received 3.5 million leva in dividends from them. — [Capital, 10 Jul 2004 (archived copy read)](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2004/07/10/227001_modelut_sofiianski/)

Audit finding
- [FACT per audit, as reported by the press] The Agency for State Internal Financial Control (АДВФК) audited Sofiyski imoti; according to the audit report as quoted in the media, the municipal budget lost "at least 33 million leva" from the company's participation in joint companies, about 10% of the 2004 city budget of 386 million leva. The audit act recorded the same violations in all the joint companies and repeatedly concluded that the company had earned no dividends or other benefit from them. — [Capital, 10 Jul 2004](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2004/07/10/227001_modelut_sofiianski/)
- [CLAIM, company's defence, quoted in the same article] The board and management "took no significant action without the sanction, knowledge and approval of the relevant body: the municipal council or an act of the mayor's administration." — [Capital, 10 Jul 2004](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2004/07/10/227001_modelut_sofiianski/)
- [FACT, already in earlier notes, repeated only for the count] Nearly 1,000 deals between April 1998 and 27 November 2003; the state financial inspection called it de facto hidden privatisation. — [Mediapool, 13 Mar 2017](https://www.mediapool.bg/megadeloto-sofiyski-imoti-priklyuchi-toshko-dobrev-e-nevinen-news261264.html)

Who approved what; moratorium
- [FACT] December 2003: the council imposed a moratorium on deals with municipal real estate; it was lifted in February 2004. During the moratorium Sofiyski imoti still completed 26 registered deals covering nearly 60 properties. — [Capital, 10 Jul 2004](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2004/07/10/227001_modelut_sofiianski/)
- [REPORTED] The moratorium was requested by Mayor Sofiyanski's group in order to stop Sofiyski imoti's deals, and was lifted three months later, again on the mayor's initiative, on the argument that hundreds of citizens' pending deals were blocked. Sofiyanski tried without success in his third term to close the company. The council did not replace the company's board. — [Capital, 6 Mar 2004 (archived copy read)](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2004/03/06/225351_desniiat_proval_v_sofiia/)
- [REPORTED] A council commission's interim report on Sofiyski imoti (early 2004) was written by one member, Vili Lilkov, from citizens' complaints and press reports, because the company refused to hand over documents. Dobrev, elected councillor in 2003, had to resign as chair of the council's architecture committee after the deals became public. — [Capital, 6 Mar 2004](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2004/03/06/225351_desniiat_proval_v_sofiia/)
- [REPORTED] In 1999 Georgi Iliev (VIS-2) asked the outgoing council to swap the VAI Holding residence at 119 Vitosha blvd for residential properties; the deal failed for lack of a quorum. — [Capital, 7 Apr 2000](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2000/04/07/255184_sofiiskite_finansovi_potainosti/)
- [OPINION/REPORTED] Capital described swaps of municipal plots, concessions, joint companies and procurement as being agreed among councillors of all parties on the principle "I back your deal if you back mine", with groups forming around business interests instead of parties ("the Sofiyanski model"). No individual councillor is named as a beneficiary of a swap. — [Capital, 21 Sep 2007 (archived copy read)](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2007/09/21/379035_suvetnici_na_kilogram/)
- [FACT] Until about 2006 council votes were recorded only as totals, so supporters of a decision stayed anonymous; after electronic voting was introduced, votes were recorded by name. — [Capital, 21 Sep 2007](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2007/09/21/379035_suvetnici_na_kilogram/)
- [FACT] 8 June 2006: in one sitting the council voted to sell off all companies still holding municipal properties. 22 February 2007 (the Thursday before publication): the council decided to start privatisation of Sofiyski imoti and several other companies. — [Capital editorial, 9 Jun 2006 (archived copy read)](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/redakcionni_komentari/2006/06/09/266303_obshtinska_banda_ad/); [Capital, 23 Feb 2007 (archived copy read)](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2007/02/23/314132_vulchi_vremena/)

When swaps stopped
- [CLAIM, Borisov] "In Sofia we have ended the swaps", said while criticising the state's land swaps on the coast and in the mountains. — [News.bg, page dated 3 Dec 2008](https://news.bg/politics/ne-se-karayte-na-es-predupredi-borisov.html)
- [REPORTED, later source] A 2019 report refers to a "political embargo on swaps" in the council, and describes later "counter-donation" deals as a way round the ban on swapping municipal property (after the period). — [Mediapool, 31 Jan 2019](https://mediapool.bg/sofiyski-imoti-se-lishava-ot-apetiten-teren-zaradi-zamenka-news289327.html)
- [UNVERIFIED, search summary only] A national law amendment banned swaps of state and municipal land, with procedures already started allowed to finish by 1 March 2009. — [Investor.bg](https://www.investor.bg/a/122-novini/74659-zabranyava-se-zamyanata-na-darzhavni-i-obshtinski-zemi) (page returned 403)

Prosecutions and outcomes
- [FACT] 24–25 August 2008: Dobrev, declared nationally wanted, was stopped at the Danube Bridge border crossing leaving for Romania and charged the next day with mismanagement over 13 preliminary sale contracts he signed, which were finalised during the moratorium; bail 20,000 leva and a travel ban. — [Capital, 29 Aug 2008 (archived copy read)](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2008/08/29/543792_hvani_me_ako_mojesh/)
- [REPORTED] The supervising prosecutor's homes were burgled three times during the investigation. A shop at 27B Moskovska St. once owned by Sofiyski imoti was sold with Dobrev's signature to a private firm, which then let it to a company 70% owned by his wife. — [Capital, 29 Aug 2008](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2008/08/29/543792_hvani_me_ako_mojesh/)
- [FACT] 3 December 2008: 13 people indicted for over 2 million leva of damage: former councillors Stanimir Zashev (board member) and Toshko Dobrev (director), former director Petar Savov, board members Milen Minchev, Stoyan Yanev, Lyubomir Ivanov, Ina Stancheva, Emiliya Bakardzhieva, and five licensed appraisers. — [Mediapool, 3 Dec 2008](https://www.mediapool.bg/osem-shefove-na-sofiyski-imoti-obvineni-za-2-mln-lv-shteti-news146623.html)
- [FACT] In 2010 Sofia City Court judge Vladimira Yaneva held one of the Sofiyski imoti cases (13 defendants including Dobrev) for about five months, then terminated it and returned it to the prosecution; the Sofia Appellate Court overturned her order and the case went back to court. In May–June 2002 she had bought two shops from Sofiyski imoti (total 64,000 leva) as proxy for her father. — [Capital, 10 Jun 2011 (archived copy read)](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2011/06/10/1104209_sofiiski_gradski_imoti/)
- [FACT, court] The 2008 case (13 preliminary contracts at understated prices, alleged damage of nearly 2 million leva): in 2012 a Sofia City Court panel chaired by Mariyan Markov acquitted Dobrev and the 13 people tried with him; the Sofia Appellate Court then terminated the case. — [Lex.bg News, 27 Feb 2018](https://news.lex.bg/%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%B2%D0%B8%D0%BD%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%8F%D1%82%D0%B0-%D0%B7%D0%B0-%D0%B0%D1%84%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B0-%D1%81%D0%BE%D1%84%D0%B8%D0%B9%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8-%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%BE/)
- [FACT, court] Sofia City Court awarded Dobrev 15,000 leva for non-pecuniary damage and 20,000 leva for legal costs against the prosecution over that case; he had filed four such claims. A May 2017 record showed 15 criminal proceedings against him in total, some still active. Board chair Simeon Simeonov was finally acquitted in this and 10 other cases and had won 41,000 leva in compensation at first instance. — [Lex.bg News, 27 Feb 2018](https://news.lex.bg/%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%B2%D0%B8%D0%BD%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%8F%D1%82%D0%B0-%D0%B7%D0%B0-%D0%B0%D1%84%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B0-%D1%81%D0%BE%D1%84%D0%B8%D0%B9%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8-%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%BE/)
- [REPORTED, tool summary] April 2012 acquittal was "the third completed trial out of seven proceedings against Dobrev and ten of his assistants and subordinates"; the prosecution had asked for seven years; the damage estimate rested entirely on one expert valuation. An appeal hearing on 29 May 2012 was postponed to 17 July because the defence counsel did not appear. — [Novinite, 19 Apr 2012](https://www.novinite.com/articles/138643/Sofia+Property+Ex+CEO+Gets+First+Acquittal+in+Court); [Novinite, 29 May 2012](https://www.novinite.com/view_news.php?id=139768)

### Inferences
- Responsibility was layered: Sofiyski imoti's management signed the deals; the council, as owner, approved or tolerated them and was controlled in 1995–2003 by the same majority that backed the mayor; the mayor's group moved against the company only from late 2003. The audit (33 million leva) and the court record (acquittals) point in different directions, so "loss to the municipality" is an audit estimate, not a judicially established figure.
- The Sofiyanski-era pattern was less "swaps" than in-kind contributions to joint companies and direct sales by the company. Swaps voted by the council itself are described by the press as common up to about 2008 but are not quantified in anything read.
- The Stoyan Yanev indicted in December 2008 as a former board member has the same name as the former chief architect; whether it is the same person was not verified.

### Gaps
- No number, area or valuation of swaps approved by the council in 1999–2008; no list of councillors who benefited; no council decision ending swaps (only Borisov's statement and a later press reference to an "embargo"). The search quota ran out before council.sofia.bg decisions could be searched.
- The АДВФК audit report itself was not located; the 33 million leva figure is as quoted by Capital from media reports of the audit.
- Individual outcomes for Zashev and the other board members are covered only by the collective "Dobrev and 13 others acquitted" statement.
- The text and date of the national ban on swaps (State Gazette) were not read.

## 2. Cleaning concessions 1997–2009 and the two conflicting cost series

### Takeaway
The three contracts were signed in 1998–1999 for 10–15 years, paid on a head-count formula unrelated to tonnage until annexes at the end of 2004, and cost the city about 50–55 million leva a year before 2005. The concessions were not terminated in 2006: a BSP motion to terminate failed in February 2007, the council settled an 8 million leva winter-cleaning dispute in July 2007, the Wolf-96 contract expired on 28 February 2009 and the mayor terminated the other two on 13 March 2009. The two cost series measure different things: payments to the concessionaires (39–62 million leva a year) versus all cleaning and waste spending (about 85 million in 2005, 80 million budgeted for 2009).

### Cited Findings
Contracts and terms
- [REPORTED] Until 1999 Sofia was cleaned by the municipal company Chistota. Half the districts stayed with that company after it was privatised by a worker-management buy-out; the rest were split between the related firms Wolf 96 OOD and DITZ AD. Concession term stated as 15 years. — [Capital, 7 Apr 2000](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2000/04/07/255184_sofiiskite_finansovi_potainosti/)
- [REPORTED] The contract for Lyulin, Ovcha kupel, Vitosha and Triaditsa was for 10 years and expired in April 2008; the Mladost contract expired in March 2009; the others ran to October 2014. All were signed while Sofiyanski was mayor and Antoan Nikolov chaired the council. — [Capital, 23 Feb 2007](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2007/02/23/314132_vulchi_vremena/)
- [Conflict] Capital in 2006 gave 1997–2009 for Wolf 96 (recorded in the earlier notes); in February 2009 it reported the Wolf-96 concession for five districts (Mladost, Ovcha kupel, Lyulin, Vitosha, part of Triaditsa) expiring at midnight on 28 February 2009; and Borisov said the Novera concession for the other 19 districts expired in 2012, not 2014. — [Capital, 20 Feb 2009 (archived copy read)](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2009/02/20/678937_vulchi_kapan/)
- [FACT, company registers as reported] Rumen Gaytanski officially held 33% of Wolf 96 and of DITZ; another 33% of each was held by Yordan Iliev (cousin of VIS-2's Georgi Iliev, manager of Wolf 96); the last third by firms from the former SIC structure (Fast Kom in Wolf 96 from the end of May 1999; Industries Group in DITZ from July 1999, three days after DITZ was named concessionaire for six districts). — [Capital, 9 Jun 2006 (archived copy read)](https://www.capital.bg/biznes/kompanii/2006/06/09/266340_otivat_li_si_mutrite_ot_biznesa_s_bokluka/)
- [REPORTED] Chistota-Sofia served the most districts; its majority stake was held by manager Plamen Barakov through bearer shares, so the real owner could not be established; he denied Gaytanski owned it. — [Capital, 9 Jun 2006](https://www.capital.bg/biznes/kompanii/2006/06/09/266340_otivat_li_si_mutrite_ot_biznesa_s_bokluka/)
- [OPINION/REPORTED] The contracts approved by the council contained no clauses allowing the city to hold the firms liable for non-performance. — [Capital, 7 Apr 2000](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2000/04/07/255184_sofiiskite_finansovi_potainosti/)
- [REPORTED] Payment was based on "наряди" (a function of the number of bins, collection frequency, workers and vehicles), so it had no direct link to the quantity of waste removed. Annexes signed at the end of 2004 replaced this with payment per container emptied and per decare cleaned, with separate prices for sweeping, snow clearing and gritting. — [Capital, 9 Jun 2006](https://www.capital.bg/biznes/kompanii/2006/06/09/266340_otivat_li_si_mutrite_ot_biznesa_s_bokluka/); [Capital, 12 Nov 2005 (archived copy read)](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2005/11/12/233708_ot_sofiiskiia_bokluk_po-skupo_nema/)
- [REPORTED] Supervision: the Sofia Inspectorate had about 290 inspectors on an average salary of 277 leva a month, about 380 bins per inspector, alongside their other duties. — [Capital, 12 Nov 2005](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2005/11/12/233708_ot_sofiiskiia_bokluk_po-skupo_nema/)

Amounts
- [REPORTED] 2000: cleaning budget set at 32 million leva against a proposed 46 million. — [Capital, 7 Apr 2000](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2000/04/07/255184_sofiiskite_finansovi_potainosti/)
- [REPORTED] 2004: cleaning costs put at 70 million leva a year. — [Capital, 10 Jul 2004](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2004/07/10/227001_modelut_sofiianski/)
- [REPORTED] Until 2005 the three firms shared on average 50–55 million leva a year for sweeping, washing, collection and haulage to Suhodol, excluding snow clearing, which was contracted separately. — [Capital, 12 Nov 2005](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2005/11/12/233708_ot_sofiiskiia_bokluk_po-skupo_nema/)
- [FACT per city hall data, as reported] The municipality paid the concessionaires between 39 million and 62 million leva a year; over 300 million leva since the contracts were signed. Equest estimated the firms' annual return at 15–20%. — [Capital, 9 Jun 2006](https://www.capital.bg/biznes/kompanii/2006/06/09/266340_otivat_li_si_mutrite_ot_biznesa_s_bokluka/)
- [REPORTED] Baling (new cost from 2005, on top of the concessions): lowest offer 29.87 leva per bale excluding VAT; about 13 million leva a year, over 38 million for the three-year contract; extra charges for unloading (8.73 leva per bale) and storage (about 12 leva per bale a year). Waste programme provisions to 2009: 6 million leva for Suhodol recultivation, 11 million for illegal dumps, 26 million for the Dolni Bogrov site. — [Capital, 12 Nov 2005](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2005/11/12/233708_ot_sofiiskiia_bokluk_po-skupo_nema/)
- [REPORTED] 2006 draft budget: 73 million leva expected from the waste fee. — [News.bg, 1 Dec 2005](https://news.bg/politics/nad-390-mln-leva-e-proektobyudzhetat-na-stolichna-obshtina-za-2006-g.html)
- [REPORTED] 2009: cleaning budget 80 million leva; Novera's prices were still those of 2004; new contracts carry a 300,000 leva penalty for poor service. — [Capital, 27 Mar 2009 (archived copy read)](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2009/03/27/696335_novite_chistachi_na_sofiia/)

2006–2007: the dispute, the failed termination and the settlement
- [REPORTED, statement in council by councillor Stefan Ivanov (SDS)] The municipality had been ordered by a court to pay nearly 3 million leva in penalties to the concessionaire for refusing to transfer 8.2 million leva for winter cleaning "last year" (winter 2005–2006). — [Capital, 23 Feb 2007](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2007/02/23/314132_vulchi_vremena/)
- [FACT] February 2007: BSP councillors Rosen Malinov and Zhelyo Boychev proposed terminating the concessions; the proposal passed two council committees; both seller and buyer threatened lawsuits for tens of millions; Equest's managing partner met Prime Minister Stanishev. At the 22 February sitting Borisov proposed a temporary commission of councillors to examine contract performance instead. SDS and Sofiyanski's group called the motion a trap. — [Capital, 9 Feb 2007 (archived copy read)](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2007/02/09/310617_vulchi_kapan/); [Capital, 23 Feb 2007](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2007/02/23/314132_vulchi_vremena/)
- [FACT] 12 July 2007: the council approved draft settlements with the three concessionaires: the city pays the principal of about 8 million leva for street cleaning in winter 2005, the firms waive late-payment interest, and the mayor is to negotiate remission of part of the court costs. The firms' request for higher collection prices was refused. — [Mediapool, 12 Jul 2007](https://www.mediapool.bg/vazmozhna-e-nova-kriza-s-bokluka-v-sofiya-news130227.html)
- [Conflict] RFE/RL's 2024 profile says the concession "was terminated in 2006 after the companies refused to clean". Contemporaneous reporting shows no termination in 2006 or 2007; the contracts ran until 2009. — [RFE/RL Bulgaria, 5 Apr 2024](https://www.svobodnaevropa.bg/a/koy-e-rumen-gaytanski-valka/32889561.html) versus [Capital, 23 Feb 2007](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2007/02/23/314132_vulchi_vremena/) and [Capital, 13 Mar 2009 (archived copy read)](https://www.capital.bg/vestnikut/kapital_prim/2009/03/13/688884_boiko_otseche_krai_na_koncesiiata/)

Sale to Equest/Novera and the 2009 end
- [REPORTED] Equest began talks in mid-2006, agreed a price before Christmas 2006 after about nine months and reportedly paid a EUR 5 million deposit; the buyer vehicle was Novera EAD, owned by Finland-registered Novera Properties Ltd. Equest planned EUR 10 million of first-year investment; the three firms had about 450 vehicles. Borisov welcomed the sale ("our only aim is a cleaner Sofia, and that requires removing Rumen Gaytanski"). — [Capital, 9 Jun 2006](https://www.capital.bg/biznes/kompanii/2006/06/09/266340_otivat_li_si_mutrite_ot_biznesa_s_bokluka/); [Capital, 9 Feb 2007](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2007/02/09/310617_vulchi_kapan/)
- [REPORTED] The tender to replace Wolf-96 in five districts was launched only in September 2008 and stalled in court; replacements (Titan AS, municipal Chistota-Iskar with subcontractor AES-X) were chosen about ten days before expiry to serve close to 600,000 residents, for one year only. Borisov refused to negotiate an extension with Novera, said Gaytanski still stood behind it and spoke of an EIB loan to buy Novera out and make it municipal. Novera said its prices had not been updated for four years and that it was not invited to the tender. — [Capital, 20 Feb 2009](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2009/02/20/678937_vulchi_kapan/)
- [FACT] 13 March 2009: the mayor sent unilateral termination notices to DITZ EAD and Chistota-Sofia AD "for systematic non-performance", effective on receipt. Twenty districts were then awarded by direct negotiation, with no firm to hold more than five districts. — [Capital, 13 Mar 2009](https://www.capital.bg/vestnikut/kapital_prim/2009/03/13/688884_boiko_otseche_krai_na_koncesiiata/); [Capital, 27 Mar 2009](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2009/03/27/696335_novite_chistachi_na_sofiia/)

### Inferences
- Resolution of the conflict flagged in the Borisov notes: "about 40 million when Borisov became mayor" (Mediapool 2025) matches the low end of the city's own range of payments to the concessionaires (39–62 million a year); "about 85 million in 2005, of which 57 million for waste" (Mediapool 2006) is total cleaning and waste spending, which from 2005 also included baling (about 13 million a year), storage, transport and landfill costs. The two series are not contradictory; they have different scope. This reading is mine; no single source reconciles them.
- Three packages are listed in Capital's 2006 graphic: (1) Vitosha, Lyulin, Mladost, Ovcha kupel, part of Triaditsa; (2) eleven districts plus part of Triaditsa; (3) Iskar, Pancharevo, Studentski, Bankya, Lozenets, Izgrev. Package 1 is Wolf 96 (confirmed by the 2009 article); package 3 has six districts, matching "DITZ was named concessionaire for six districts"; package 2 would then be Chistota-Sofia. The firm labels were lost in the archived text, so packages 2 and 3 are inferred.
- Signing dates: Wolf 96 about April 1998 (10 years to April 2008, apparently extended to February 2009), DITZ July 1999, Chistota-Sofia 1999 (15 years to October 2014). The earlier notes' "1997" for Wolf 96 rests on one Capital sentence and conflicts with the 10-year/April 2008 statement.
- Under Sofiyanski: contracts awarded without effective penalty clauses and paid on inputs for about six years; the pricing fix (annexes) also came under him at the end of 2004. Under Borisov: no re-tender prepared in time for the known February 2009 expiry.

### Gaps
- The contracts, the council decisions awarding them and the 2004 annexes were not read; all terms are from Capital.
- The court judgment on the 8.2 million leva winter-cleaning claim (court, date, exact award) was not found; it is known only from a councillor's statement and the July 2007 settlement.
- Yearly payments to the concessionaires for each year 1998–2008 were not found, only the 39–62 million range and the 300 million cumulative figure.
- No audit of the concessions by the Audit Office or the financial inspection was found.

## 3. Municipal finances 1996–2009: budget, debt, Obshtinska banka, ratings

### Takeaway
The 1999 Eurobond matured on 3 June 2002 and cost 97.8 million leva to redeem, of which 76.5 million was budgeted; no refinancing loan was confirmed. Budget points found: 416 million leva of spending in 2000 (with a 50 million shortfall), 386 million in 2004, a draft of over 390 million for 2006. Obshtinska banka began work in 1996; the municipality kept about 67%, but in 2004–2005 a blocking stake of about 26% passed to firms of Hristo Kovachki through former municipal companies, and the municipality regained control only after central-bank action in 2011.

## 4. Urban planning 1995–2009: General Urban Plan, green areas, chief architects

### Takeaway
The plan was drafted by the municipal architecture directorate under chief architect Stoyan Yanev around 2004, went to parliament almost unchanged, and has applied since 28 January 2007. The national Ombudsman's August 2006 opinion found reduced protection for green areas and built-in over-densification but still urged quick adoption. No official figure for hectares of green space lost was found; the one hard number is that from January 2007 to September 2020 the city expropriated no private plot for a park, garden or inter-block green space.

### Cited Findings
- [REPORTED] The plan "was prepared two years ago" (about 2004) by the municipality's architecture directorate under then chief architect Stoyan Yanev, and the draft before parliament "has hardly been changed since". — [Mediapool, 3 Aug 2006](https://www.mediapool.bg/gradoustroistveniyat-plan-na-sofiya-pozvolyava-proizvol-i-koruptsiya-news120088.html)
- [FACT, institutional opinion] Ombudsman Ginyo Ganev, 3 August 2006: the draft in places conflicts with the Constitution; protection of green areas is reduced; over-densification of neighbourhoods is built in; building regimes differ from those in the rest of the country. Building in green areas would be allowed for two more types of structure, one of them "recreation facilities serving tourists", which could be whole hotels. The plan assumed 1,390,000 residents by 2020 while the population was already 1,377,000 in May 2006. He recommended keeping the strict regime of the Spatial Planning Act for green areas, a public council, and a referendum on improving the plan within a year of adoption. — [Mediapool, 3 Aug 2006](https://www.mediapool.bg/gradoustroistveniyat-plan-na-sofiya-pozvolyava-proizvol-i-koruptsiya-news120088.html)
- [REPORTED] The same opinion stressed the need to adopt the plan urgently "to bring more transparency, stability and predictability". — [Capital, 8 Sep 2006 (archived copy read)](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2006/09/08/281211_edin_bolen_grad/)
- [CLAIM, BSP councillor arch. Borisov] About EUR 1 billion would be needed just to expropriate already restituted plots inside the planned green system. Another BSP councillor: "Better a bad plan than no plan; we saw what Sofia came to look like in the last 10 years with these short-term extensions." — [Mediapool, 3 Aug 2006](https://www.mediapool.bg/gradoustroistveniyat-plan-na-sofiya-pozvolyava-proizvol-i-koruptsiya-news120088.html)
- [REPORTED, unnamed industry sources] A small group of intermediaries bought plots, had the zoning changed to the most intensive use through contacts in the municipality and sold the "projects" on for seven- and eight-figure euro sums; all formally legal under amendments to the old plan. — [Capital, 8 Sep 2006](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2006/09/08/281211_edin_bolen_grad/)
- [FACT] 16 February 2006: parliament's environment committee backed a moratorium on building in green areas and inter-block spaces in Sofia until the plan was adopted, then widened it to all towns and set up working groups. New chief architect Petar Dikov supported a ban, explaining that some restitution laws automatically revive an old building right even where the plot is now inside a garden or park. He said the general plan does not itself regulate building in inter-block spaces, and that "inter-block space" was not defined in law. — [Mediapool, 16 Feb 2006](https://www.mediapool.bg/moratorium-za-stroezhi-v-gradinkite-nadvisna-nad-sofiya-news114439.html)
- [FACT] The same day Borisov ordered a stop to a five-storey residential building in Krasna polyana that residents said stood on a playground and green space; a check by the municipal architecture directorate found the building permit had been issued in breach of the Spatial Planning Act. Deputies said the municipality already had legal powers to stop such building and that the mayor was passing his problem to parliament. — [Mediapool, 16 Feb 2006](https://www.mediapool.bg/moratorium-za-stroezhi-v-gradinkite-nadvisna-nad-sofiya-news114439.html)
- [FACT] Dikov became chief architect on 9 February 2006 after a competition and left on 9 February 2016. — [Mediapool, 17 Nov 2015](https://www.mediapool.bg/glavniyat-arhitekt-na-sofiya-se-ottegli-sled-10-godini-sluzhba-news241920.html)
- [FACT, mayor's written answer as reported] From 28 January 2007 to 1 September 2020 the municipality expropriated no private property under the provision for parks, gardens and street or inter-block greenery ("a round zero"); it expropriated 100 properties totalling 300 decares under the "other special purpose" provision (cemetery parks, protective planting and similar) for 18,107,146 leva. — [Sega, 26 Oct 2020](https://www.segabg.com/node/157510)

### Inferences
- The overbuilding of the late 1990s and early 2000s took place under the pre-2007 plan and its piecemeal amendments, i.e. under Sofiyanski and chief architect Yanev; the new plan was drafted under the same team and adopted under Borisov and Dikov with little change. Both administrations share the outcome.
- The 2006 Krasna polyana case is a documented instance (the municipality's own check) of a building permit issued unlawfully on green space, but it is one case, not a statistic.
- Zero expropriations for parks over 13 years means the green system drawn in the plan stayed largely on private land; this is a failure to implement that starts in the Borisov term and continues under Fandakova.

### Gaps
- No official figure for hectares of green area lost, or square metres of green space per resident, for any year 1995–2009.
- The Ombudsman's opinion was read only through Mediapool and Capital; the Ombudsman's page is script-rendered and could not be extracted.
- Why and when Yanev left office, and whether any proceedings concerned his time as chief architect, were not found. A Stoyan Yanev was indicted in December 2008 as a former board member of Sofiyski imoti (section 1); identity with the chief architect is unverified.
- Council votes on the draft plan (2006) and on the 2009 amendment were not found.
- No statistics on building permits or on plan amendments granted per year.

## 5. Other municipal services 1995–2009: public transport, stray dogs, kindergartens, roads

### Takeaway
The best-documented new item is the rigged tender for private bus lines: a meeting recorded in 2007 and published in 2008 showed the deputy mayor for transport agreeing in advance which operators would get which lines, and he resigned. Kindergarten admissions in February 2008 left about 5,000 children without a place. Little new was found on fleet purchases, fares before 2008 or completed roads.

Public transport
- [REPORTED] In 2007 deputy mayor for transport Velizar Stoilov was recorded at a meeting in the offices of the municipal bus company agreeing in advance which bus lines would go to which private operators; the 39-minute recording was published by the weekly Tema in 2008 and led to his departure (Capital in 2008 says he resigned; in 2016 it says he was dismissed). ## 6. Toplofikatsiya Sofia 1995–2009: ownership, debts, the Valentin Dimitrov cases

### Takeaway
Ownership changed four times: municipal in the 1990s, 58% municipal and 42% state from 2002 (state took its stake against gas debts), 100% state from 2008 (the council gave up its 58% for free), and 100% municipal again from 2010. Debts reached 510 million leva by end-2009. Of the criminal cases against director Valentin Dimitrov, the 2009 sentence of 14 years was overturned, and the only final conviction found is three years for mismanagement, upheld on appeal in February 2015.

### Cited Findings
Ownership and financing
- [REPORTED] In 1996 the company was owned by Sofia Municipality; Dimitrov joined its management board in July 1996 and became its chair three months later; he was executive director from 2002 to 2006. — [Capital, 25 Jan 2013 (archived copy read)](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2013/01/25/1991149_erata_na_toploto/)
- [REPORTED] 2002: the state entered as owner of 42% in exchange for the company's debts to Bulgargaz. — [Capital, 10 Sep 2010 (archived copy read)](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2010/09/10/958536_topli_pari/)
- [FACT, loan agreement] 18 June 2003: World Bank loan 4704-BUL of EUR 26 million to Toplofikacia Sofia EAD, described as "jointly owned by the Municipality and the Guarantor" (the Republic of Bulgaria), guaranteed by the state; alongside an EBRD loan of EUR 30 million and a Kozloduy decommissioning fund grant of up to EUR 30 million (agreement of 14 Aug 2002). The recitals record two Sofia Municipality decisions, of 28 September 2001 and 15 April 2002, to bring in a private operator for heating services. — [World Bank, Loan Agreement (District Heating Project), 18 Jun 2003](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/767621468016766159/pdf/L147041sofia0LA.pdf)
- [REPORTED] 2008: with the municipality at 58% and the BSP-led government at 42%, the state prevailed and Sofia transferred its stake to the state free of charge because of the debts to Bulgargaz; the council voted unanimously except for BSP's Georgi Kadiev. Borisov later called the then government's conduct "racketeering"; former minister Petar Dimitrov said it was the only way to avoid bankruptcy. — [Capital, 10 Sep 2010](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2010/09/10/958536_topli_pari/)
- [REPORTED] 2010: Prime Minister Borisov's government transferred the company back to the municipality free of charge, with both assets and liabilities, although the council chair had wanted it without the debts to Bulgargaz and BEH. — [Capital, 10 Sep 2010](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2010/09/10/958536_topli_pari/)
- [Conflict] Bulgarian Wikipedia says the company was a state-owned single-shareholder company from 1993 until the 2010 transfer; this omits the municipal ownership documented by the World Bank agreement and Capital. — [Bulgarian Wikipedia, Топлофикация София](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Топлофикация_София)

Debts
- [FACT per company accounts cited in a municipal document, as reported] End-2009: liabilities 510.34 million leva; current receivables 416.634 million leva; collection rate a little over 50%; loss 43 million leva; plus a recognised debt of 214.963 million leva to BEH, assigned by Bulgargaz in early 2009. The auditor warned that fixed assets might be overstated. — [Capital, 10 Sep 2010](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2010/09/10/958536_topli_pari/)
- [OPINION, Capital] Owners treated the 400,000 subscribers as voters: heat was sold below cost and receivables were not collected. — [Capital, 10 Sep 2010](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2010/09/10/958536_topli_pari/)
- [REPORTED] About a month after the Dimitrov scandal broke in 2006, the council gave New Energy Vision AD, without tender, the right to build a heat plant worth over EUR 30 million at a Toplofikatsiya site; NDSV councillor Vladimir Karolev said it would cost the city millions in lost value at any future privatisation; the proposal came from his party colleague Atanas Tasev. — [Capital, 21 Sep 2007](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2007/09/21/379035_suvetnici_na_kilogram/)

Dimitrov cases (additions to the chronology in the Borisov notes)
- [FACT, court; tool summary] 2008: Sofia Regional Court convicted him under the Currency Act for not declaring deals of over 10 million leva (reported as five years). 22 December 2011: Sofia City Court revoked that judgment in full and returned the case to the prosecution for flaws in the indictment. — [Novinite, 22 Dec 2011](https://www.novinite.com/articles/135091/5-Year+Prison+Term+for+Ex+Sofia+Heating+Utility+Boss+Revoked)
- [REPORTED] The currency case concerned transactions of 4,868,155 leva in Kufstein, Austria, between 20 Oct 1999 and 25 Jun 2003; the file "disappeared" in the city court for three years. — [Capital, 25 Jan 2013](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2013/01/25/1991149_erata_na_toploto/)
- [FACT, court] Summer 2009: judge Georgi Kolev sentenced him to 14 years and confiscation of all property for draining nearly 6 million leva; the reasoning was copied from the indictment and the sentence was overturned on appeal. — [Capital, 25 Jan 2013](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2013/01/25/1991149_erata_na_toploto/)

### Inferences
- Dimitrov ran the company's boards for ten years (1996–2006), nearly all of it under Sofiyanski, with the municipality as sole or majority owner; the conduct later punished (2003–2006) falls almost wholly before Borisov. Borisov's administration removed him within five months.
- The 2008 handover of the municipal stake and its return in 2010 were political decisions on both sides; the council voted for each. The debt (510 million leva at end-2009) is an inherited and shared liability, built up since the late 1990s.
- The Novinite summaries partly conflict with each other on dates (one places the arrest in 2005; the established date is July 2006) and should be used only for the court events listed.

### Gaps
- Year-by-year debt and receivables for 1995–2008 were not found; a "503 million leva owed to BEH, accumulated since 1998" figure was seen only in a search summary of a 2014 Capital article and is unverified.
- The exact date and legal act of the 2002 state entry, the 2008 transfer and the 2010 return (council decision numbers, cabinet decisions) were not read.
- Whether the private-operator plan of 2001–2002 went any further was not found.
- The final outcome of the money-laundering case (seven years at first instance, August 2022) and of the main embezzlement case after 2013 remains unconfirmed.

## 7. Concessions and deals approved by the council: parking, kiosks, joint companies and others

### Takeaway
No parking concession had been awarded by mid-2008: towing and clamping were done by the municipal company Parkingi i garazhi itself, which reported 11 million leva of revenue, about 2 million leva paid to the budget, and losses on both towing and clamping. Other deals found are press-reported: an exclusive kiosk supplier, a no-tender heat-plant right in 2006 and the cross-party vote on Obshtinska banka. Nothing new was found on the Sofiyska voda vote, billboards, markets or airport land.

## 8. Snow-clearing and other failures with an institutional finding

### Takeaway
No court ruling, audit or inspectorate finding against the municipality on snow clearing was found. The only court-related item is the opposite: a court award against the municipality for withholding payment to the cleaning firms for winter 2005–2006, settled by the council in July 2007.

### Cited Findings
- [REPORTED, councillor's statement in council] The municipality was ordered to pay nearly 3 million leva in penalties for refusing to pay 8.2 million leva for winter cleaning in 2005–2006. — [Capital, 23 Feb 2007](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2007/02/23/314132_vulchi_vremena/)
- [FACT] 12 July 2007: council approved a settlement to pay the principal (about 8 million leva) with interest waived. — [Mediapool, 12 Jul 2007](https://www.mediapool.bg/vazmozhna-e-nova-kriza-s-bokluka-v-sofiya-news130227.html)
- [REPORTED] Winter snow-clearing "scandals" around 1999–2000 exposed that a SIC-linked firm had acquired 33.1% of Wolf 96. Snow clearing was not in the base concession price and was contracted separately each year. — [Capital, 7 Apr 2000](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2000/04/07/255184_sofiiskite_finansovi_potainosti/); [Capital, 12 Nov 2005](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2005/11/12/233708_ot_sofiiskiia_bokluk_po-skupo_nema/)
- [FACT, municipal check] February 2006: the municipality's architecture directorate found that a building permit in Krasna polyana had been issued in breach of the Spatial Planning Act (see section 4). — [Mediapool, 16 Feb 2006](https://www.mediapool.bg/moratorium-za-stroezhi-v-gradinkite-nadvisna-nad-sofiya-news114439.html)

- The city withheld the winter 2005–2006 payment (the first winter under Borisov) presumably as a sanction for poor work, but lost in court and paid in full. ### Gaps

## 9. National Audit Office and State Financial Inspection findings not already recorded

### Takeaway
One audit finding is new to the notes: the state internal financial control agency's audit of Sofiyski imoti (reported 2004) with a loss of at least 33 million leva from joint companies. The Audit Office's summary report on municipal property disposals in 1998–2005 covers Sofia together with 18 other municipalities and gives no Sofia-specific numbers.

### Cited Findings
- [FACT per audit, as reported] АДВФК audit of Sofiyski imoti: at least 33 million leva lost from joint companies; identical violations in each; no dividends earned (details in section 1). — [Capital, 10 Jul 2004](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2004/07/10/227001_modelut_sofiianski/)
- [FACT, audit report read in full] Audit Office summary report on disposal of private municipal real estate, 1 Jan 1998 – 31 Dec 2005, in 19 municipalities including Sofia: 73% of the audited municipalities had no adopted programme for managing and disposing of municipal property; council decisions were taken case by case; the Municipal Property Act made auction the main method of sale but set no criteria for which properties must go to auction, so auctions were not widely used; neither the Act nor local ordinances assigned duties and responsibility to the bodies deciding on disposals, which "creates a risk of inefficiency and conditions for corrupt practices"; councils decided without prior criteria separating properties to be privatised, contributed in kind or sold under the Act. The report gives findings in general terms and names Sofia only in the list of audited municipalities. — [National Audit Office, summary report](https://www.bulnao.government.bg/bg/documents/149/Obobshten-obshtini08.doc)
- [REPORTED] Parkingi i garazhi, as a private-law municipal company, lay outside the Audit Office's remit in 2008. — [Capital, 20 Jun 2008](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2008/06/20/516699_sinia_zona_ili_cherna_dupka/)
- [FACT, private audit ordered by the municipality] Ernst & Young audit of Obshtinska banka: about 26 million leva lent to parties related to the minority shareholder without risk analysis (covers the period up to 2011; see section 3). — [Capital, 17 Jul 2012](https://www.capital.bg/biznes/finansi/2012/07/17/1867532_obshtinska_banka_kato_istoriia_bez_krai/)

### Inferences
- The Audit Office's general findings (no disposal programme, case-by-case council decisions, little use of auctions, no assigned responsibility) describe exactly the conditions under which Sofia's joint-company and swap deals took place, but the published summary does not say which findings applied to Sofia.
- Municipal companies, where most of the disputed deals were done (Sofiyski imoti, Parkingi i garazhi), were largely outside the Audit Office's reach; the only public audit of one was by the financial control agency.

### Gaps
- The Sofia-specific audit report behind the 19-municipality summary was not found on bulnao.government.bg.
- The 2005 audits of the water concession and metro construction were not located as documents (still known only from the Audit Office president's interview).
- No Audit Office or financial inspection (АДФИ) report on Sofia's budget, procurement, waste fee or companies for 2006–2009 was found; the archive could not be searched further after the search quota ran out.
