# Sofia Municipality — hard time-series indicators 1996–2026 (for comparing mayoral periods 1995–2005, 2005–2009, 2009–2023, 2023–present)

Notes for the writer (apply to every section):
- Research date 14 Nov 2026. BGN figures are nominal. Bulgaria adopted the euro on 1 Jan 2026 at the fixed rate 1 EUR = 1.95583 BGN; 2026 figures are in EUR, and the municipality itself now restates 2023–2025 budgets in EUR.
- "Eurostat city statistics" = Eurostat Urban Audit data for city code BG001C (Sofia), pulled directly from the Eurostat API on 14 Nov 2026. These are official but some series have visible breaks/odd values; flagged where seen.
- Several press items (Capital.bg, minfin.bg) could not be opened (HTTP 403); where a number comes only from a search-result snippet it is marked "(snippet)".
- Almost nothing was found for 1996–2000 for most indicators; this is stated per section rather than estimated.

## 1. Sofia metro: km, stations, openings, ridership

### Takeaway
The metro went from nothing in 1996 to 8 stations/~10 km at end-2005, 14 stations at end-2009, 47 stations/52 km at end-2023 and 50 stations/55 km after the 14 Aug 2026 opening; the bulk of the network (33 of 50 stations) opened in 2012–2021. Ridership is reported as 94 m (2023) and 127.2 m (2024), but the size of that jump is not explained and sources disagree.

### Inferences
- Boundary values: 1996 = 0 km/0 stations (under construction); Nov 2005 = 8 stations, ~9.9 km (8.1 + 1.8); Nov 2009 = 14 stations, roughly 18 km (9.9 + 5.7 + the Serdika–Stadium link; en.wiki gives 4.8 km + 3.2 km of tunnels for the two 2005–06 construction lots, i.e. ~8 km added); Nov 2023 = 47 stations, 52 km; Nov 2026 = 50 stations, 55 km.
- Stations opened per mayoral period: 1995–2005: 8; 2005–2009: 6; 2009–2023: 33; 2023–present: 3. Note that sections opened in 2009 were started in 2005–06 and the 2026 section was contracted before Nov 2023, so openings lag the decision by one term.
- The 94 m → 127.2 m jump 2023→2024 without any new stations likely reflects a counting change (e.g. new ticketing/validation system) rather than real growth; treat with caution.

### Gaps
- No year-by-year official ridership series (Metropolitan EAD / NSI) was retrieved for 1998–2022; metropolitan.bg was not successfully queried.
- Exact km at 2015, 2016, 2020, 2021 are not individually sourced (only 48 km in Sept 2020 and 52 km in 2021–25).

## 2. Municipal budget: totals, capital expenditure, execution

### Takeaway
The adopted budget framework grew from roughly BGN 0.4 bn (2006 draft) to BGN 1.27 bn (2012), ~BGN 1.5 bn (2014–15), BGN 2.56 bn (2023) and EUR 1.54 bn (≈BGN 3.0 bn) in 2026 — all nominal. Capital spending peaked around 2014–15 (BGN ~700 m, metro/EU-funded), fell sharply in 2016, and is about one fifth of the 2026 budget; S&P describes capital-plan implementation as "generally weak".

## 3. Municipal debt and credit ratings

### Takeaway
Direct debt was BGN 650 m (2021), 639 m (2022) and ~655 m (2023e) — about 30–40% of operating revenues, which S&P calls a low debt burden; Sofia alone holds roughly half of all Bulgarian municipal debt. S&P is the only agency found rating Sofia: BBB- (investment grade) from Dec 2017, BBB Dec 2019, BBB+ July 2025 — always capped at the sovereign rating.

### Inferences
- Applying the MoF shares gives Sofia debt of roughly BGN 590 m (Mar 2016) and BGN 630 m (Mar 2018) — i.e. nominal direct debt has been flat at ~BGN 600–650 m for 2016–2023 while revenues nearly doubled, so the debt/revenue ratio fell.
- The "investment grade since 2010" and "first investment grade in Dec 2017" reports reconcile if Sofia was BBB- from 2010, cut to BB+ when the sovereign was cut (Dec 2014) and restored in Dec 2017. This sovereign-cap explanation is from background knowledge and was not sourced in this session.
- Because the rating is capped at the sovereign, upgrades in 2017, 2019 and 2025 track Bulgaria's rating more than any mayor's decisions; the stand-alone scores (management = 4 "weak") are the more discriminating metric.

## 4. Population, GDP per capita, wages, unemployment

### Takeaway
Population of Sofia (capital) district rose from ~1.19 m in the mid-1990s to ~1.30 m in 2025; GDP per capita rose from EUR 3,000 (2000) to EUR 35,400 (2024), i.e. from 50% to 143% of the EU-27 average in PPS and consistently about 2.1–2.3× the national figure. These are economy-wide outcomes only weakly attributable to the municipality.

## 11. Local tax rates

### Takeaway
Evidence found shows rates were largely left unchanged for long periods: the building (property) tax stood at 1.875‰ (far below the 4.5‰ legal ceiling) from at least 2010 to 2023, and the main documented changes were the 2009/2010 base increase, a 2020 rise in the property-transfer tax from 2.5% to 3%, and a vehicle-tax increase. S&P notes the city "has been reluctant" to use its tax flexibility.

### Cited Findings
- Property (building) tax 1.875‰ of tax valuation as of Dec 2019, with the legal maximum 4.5‰; 2019 changes did not touch it — [Capital, 9 Dec 2019](https://www.capital.bg/biznes/imoti/2019/12/09/4003203_po-visokiiat_danuk_buta_sdelkite_s_imoti_kum_siviia/); still 1.875‰ in the council ordinance text and in 2023 explanations — [Ciela Norma, SOS ordinance on local taxes](https://www.ciela.net/svobodna-zona-normativi/view/2135585788); [Darik](https://darik.bg/kak-se-opredela-kolko-danak-sgradi-i-taksa-smet-plastame)

## 12. Citizen satisfaction — EC "Quality of Life in European Cities" perception surveys

### Takeaway
Between 2006 and 2015 satisfaction with public transport tripled (23% → 74%) and with green spaces rose from 25% to 66%, then both plateaued or slipped by 2023; air-quality satisfaction was stuck at ~26% until 2019 and jumped to 40% in 2023; cleanliness (≈30–35%) and perceived corruption/administrative efficiency have barely moved. ## 13. EU funds absorbed

### Takeaway
No clean split by programming period was found.
