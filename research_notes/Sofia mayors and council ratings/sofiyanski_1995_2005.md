# Stefan Sofiyanski as mayor of Sofia (1995–2005) and the 2005 acting mayor

Reading notes for the report writer:

- Labels used below: **[FACT]** = official record, court outcome, audit finding, signed contract or operator's own data; **[REPORTED]** = stated as fact by a news outlet but not checked against a primary document; **[ALLEGATION]** = prosecution charge, opponent's claim or unproven press claim; **[SELF-CLAIM]** = the mayor's own statement; **[OPINION]** = commentary.
- Access limits during this research: capital.bg, mi2003.cik.bg (Central Election Commission 2003 archive), dnevnik.bg, sofia.bg and web.archive.org all refused automated fetches (HTTP 403 / blocked). Where a Capital article is cited, the content comes from a search-engine summary of that page, not from a full read, and is marked "(snippet only)". - All money figures are in the currency the source used. Pre-July-1999 leva are "old" leva (1,000 old = 1 new).

## 1. Term dates, backing, election results, departure, acting mayor

### Takeaway
Sofiyanski held office from 19 November 1995 to 29 June 2005, winning three elections (1995 as SDS in a runoff; 1999 as SDS/ODS in the first round with about 69%; 2003 as leader of his own party SSD in a runoff with about 54%). He left mid-term to take a seat in parliament after the June 2005 general election, in the middle of the Suhodol waste crisis and with three criminal cases pending; deputy mayor Minko Gerdzhikov acted as mayor from 30 June 2005 until Boyko Borisov took office in November 2005 (about 4.5 months).

### Cited Findings
- [FACT] Term: 19 November 1995 – 29 June 2005. Predecessor Aleksandar Yanchulev (SDS, 21 Oct 1991 – 18 Nov 1995); successor as elected mayor Boyko Borisov from 10 November 2005. — [bg.wikipedia, Кмет на София](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Кмет_на_София); [bg.wikipedia, Стефан Софиянски](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Стефан_Софиянски)
- [FACT] Party: SDS member 1989–2001; founded the Union of Free Democrats (ССД/SSD) in early December 2001 after criticising the SDS leadership from mid-2001. — [bg.wikipedia](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Стефан_Софиянски); [omda.bg profile](https://www.omda.bg/page.php?IDMenu=375&IDArticle=1032)
- [FACT] He was simultaneously caretaker prime minister February–May 1997 (appointed by President Petar Stoyanov) while remaining mayor. — [omda.bg profile](https://www.omda.bg/page.php?IDMenu=375&IDArticle=1032)
- **1995 election.** [FACT] SDS candidate; won the runoff on 12 November 1995 against Ventsislav Yosifov, backed by BSP. — [bg.wikipedia](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Стефан_Софиянски). [REPORTED] Re-elected in the first round with about 69% against BSP's Rumen Ovcharov — [bg.wikipedia](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Стефан_Софиянски); split given as 69% : 23% — [Frognews](https://frognews.bg/glasat-na-jabata/glasat/stroiteli-kapitalizma-stefan-sofiianski-zlatnata-sinia-kokoshka.html)

- [REPORTED] National local-election turnout: 51.6% in 1999 and 47.0% in 2003 (national/Sofia scope not confirmed; from a table on geopolitique.eu surfaced in search). — [geopolitique.eu](https://geopolitique.eu/en/articles/municipal-election-in-sofia-29-october-2023/)

- [REPORTED] During the January 2005 crisis Kostov's DSB demanded his resignation and tabled it at the municipal council. — [Novinite](https://www.novinite.com/articles/43530/Agreement+Reached+on+Sofia+Garbage+Dump) (outcome of the vote not found)

- [FACT] In early July 2005 it was acting mayor Gerdzhikov, not Sofiyanski, who declared the crisis situation over the Suhodol blockade. — [RFE/RL Bulgaria](https://bulgaria.rferl.org/a/sofiyanski-borisov-gaytanski-valka-krizi-bokluk-sofiya/33561569.html)
- [REPORTED] Borisov won the partial election in October/November 2005 as an independent, beating BSP's Tatyana Doncheva in the runoff with about 68–68.5% to 31%, with 223,577 votes. — [epicenter.bg](https://epicenter.bg/article/Borisov-stana-kmet-prez-2005-g--s-223-577-glasa--Hekimyan-srina-GERB-do-18-598/331068/11/0)

### Inferences
- The mandate eroded sharply: a first-round landslide in 1999 (about 69%) versus about 30% in round one and a 54% runoff in 2003, after his break with SDS.
- Of roughly 9 years 7 months in office, he was suspended by court order for about 12 weeks (5 May – 29 July 2004).
- The departure was voluntary and not the result of a conviction; the timing coincided with both the waste crisis and the availability of parliamentary immunity. Which motive dominated is disputed (his own account stresses the waste crisis; critics stress immunity).

### Gaps
- Exact vote counts, percentages to one decimal, and Sofia-specific turnout for 1995, 1999 and 2003 were not obtained from a primary source (CIK archive blocked). One search summary gave "279,476 votes" for Sofiyanski in 1999 round one and "29.4% vs 27.9%" for 2003 round one, but I could not confirm which page these came from: treat as unverified.
- RFE/RL's article (as summarised) says he "resigned in April 2005"; other sources give 29 June 2005 as the end of term. The exact date and form of the resignation (council decision number) were not found.
- Who acted as mayor during the May–July 2004 suspension was not found.
- Whether Gerdzhikov was formally elected by the Sofia Municipal Council, and the decision number, was not confirmed.

## 2. Concrete achievements (metro, infrastructure, finance)

### Takeaway
The best-documented achievement is the opening of the Sofia metro: 5 stations/6.5 km on 28 January 1998, growing to 8 stations/10 km by April 2003, plus the Japanese-loan financing that funded the next section. Sofia was also the first Bulgarian municipality to issue a Eurobond (about EUR 50 million, 1999). Evidence on roads and other works is mostly descriptive, without cost or outcome data.

### Cited Findings
- [FACT] 28 January 1998: first metro section commissioned, 5 stations, 6.5 km (Slivnitsa blvd – Lyulin – K. Velichkov). — [Metropolitan, History of Metro Extensions](https://www.metropolitan.bg/en/op-transport-extension/istoriya-razvitie-metro)
- [FACT] 17 September 1999: Opalchenska station opened; 31 October 2000: Serdika station (Sv. Nedelya sq.) opened, giving 8.1 km and 7 stations. — [Metropolitan](https://www.metropolitan.bg/en/op-transport-extension/istoriya-razvitie-metro)
- [FACT] 20 April 2003: 1.8 km extension to Obelya; total 10 km and 8 stations. — [Metropolitan](https://www.metropolitan.bg/en/op-transport-extension/istoriya-razvitie-metro)
- [FACT] The Sv. Nedelya – Vasil Levski Stadium section (2.3 km central part, 2 stations) was financed by a Sofia Municipality loan from the Japan Bank for International Cooperation; construction started in 2005 and it opened in 2009 (after his term). — [Metropolitan](https://www.metropolitan.bg/en/op-transport-extension/istoriya-razvitie-metro)
- [SELF-CLAIM] He cites the metro tender won by two Japanese companies as an example of a transparent procedure. — [paragraf.bg](https://paragraf.bg/stefan-sofiyanski-otteglih-se-kato-kmet-zaradi-krizata-s-bokluka)
- [REPORTED] First term: public investment resumed from 1997 after the 1996–97 crisis; Todor Aleksandrov blvd built above the metro. Second term: central street renovations, Akad. Ivan Geshov blvd, new Central Bus Station. — [bg.wikipedia](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Стефан_Софиянски) (citing Boris Angelov, "Кметовете на София", 2015, pp. 151–154)
- [REPORTED] 12 May 1999: Sofia Municipality's Eurobonds sold, raising about EUR 50 million for 3 years at 9.75%. — [FLGR, Innovative practices in Bulgaria](https://www.flgr.bg/en/innovations/?iid=161) (secondary, loosely translated source)
- [FACT] Sofiyska Voda concession (signed 23 Dec 1999, effective 6 Oct 2000) brought a private operator with an investment obligation of 150 million over 25 years (currency not stated in the source), and an EBRD loan of EUR 31 million signed December 2000 for network rehabilitation serving 1.2 million people. — [Audit Office president interview, Dnevnik 11 Oct 2005](https://bulnao.government.bg/bg/documents/12003/interview-dnevnik111005.doc); [Bankwatch](https://bankwatch.org/?p=111927). See section 4 for the negative side.
- [REPORTED, snippet only] Obshtinska banka: Sofia Municipality held about 67.5–67.65% of the bank; it was put up for privatisation in 2017 "21 years after its creation" (i.e. founded about 1996, during his first term). — [Capital 2017](https://www.capital.bg/biznes/kompanii/2017/07/27/3014278_ot_obshtinska_kum_chastna_banka/); [Capital 2012](https://www.capital.bg/biznes/finansi/2012/07/17/1867532_obshtinska_banka_kato_istoriia_bez_krai/)
- [REPORTED, snippet only] In 2005 businessman Hristo Kovachki acquired a 26% blocking stake in Obshtinska banka, complicating municipal control. — [Capital, 2 July 2005](https://www.capital.bg/biznes/kompanii/2005/07/02/231991_hristo_kovachki_izkupi_26_ot_obshtinska_banka/)
- [SELF-CLAIM] Garbage collection cost 65 million leva a year out of a municipal budget of 400 million leva in his time. — [paragraf.bg](https://paragraf.bg/stefan-sofiyanski-otteglih-se-kato-kmet-zaradi-krizata-s-bokluka)

### Inferences
- The metro went from zero to 10 km in his tenure, but most of that tunnel work had been under construction since the socialist period; his contribution was finishing, financing and opening it. No source read here quantifies the share built before 1995.
- A 9.75% three-year Eurobond in 1999 was expensive money by later standards but was a first market access for a Bulgarian municipality two years after hyperinflation; whether it was repaid on time (2002) was not confirmed.

### Gaps
- No verified figures for municipal privatisation revenue, kilometres of roads rebuilt, public transport fleet renewal, or the cost of the 1998–2003 metro sections.
- Eurobond repayment, use of proceeds and any audit of it were not found. Capital's April 2000 article "Софийските финансови потайности" appears to criticise the opacity of municipal finances but could not be read (403): [link](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2000/04/07/255184_sofiiskite_finansovi_potainosti/).
- Exact founding date and the municipality's initial stake in Obshtinska banka not verified against BNB or the bank.

## 3. Criminal cases against Sofiyanski and the "Sofiyski imoti" affair

### Takeaway
Sofiyanski was charged in 2004 in three cases of abuse of office over municipal deals (Central Halls, Millennium Center land, Gurko 1 business centre) and was acquitted in all three at first instance in 2006–2008; I found no conviction. Separately, the municipal company Sofiyski imoti carried out nearly 1,000 property deals in 1998–2003, which a 2003 State Financial Inspection report called "hidden privatisation"; the prosecutions there targeted its director Toshko Dobrev, not the mayor, and ended mostly in acquittals or limitation.

### Cited Findings
**Cases against Sofiyanski personally**
- [FACT] 2004: Sofia City Prosecutor's Office examined four municipal deals and filed three cases for crimes in office. — [bg.wikipedia](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Стефан_Софиянски)
- [ALLEGATION, prosecution] Central Halls (Централни хали): undervaluing the derelict building and land roughly 400 times when forming a joint company with Israeli firm Ashtrom. — [Mediapool, 2 July 2004](https://www.mediapool.bg/s-treto-obvinenie-sofiyanski-mozhe-zavinagi-da-bade-otstranen-ot-kmetstvoto-news9794.html)
- [ALLEGATION, prosecution] Millennium business centre: about 7,000 sq m in Ivan Vazov quarter given to OBB free of charge (the article dates this to 1993, i.e. before his term; this looks like a possible error in the source and should be re-checked), and the 2001 sale of the municipality's 25% stake back to OBB at a low price; alleged damage about USD 5 million (land) and USD 32,000 (shares). — [Mediapool](https://www.mediapool.bg/s-treto-obvinenie-sofiyanski-mozhe-zavinagi-da-bade-otstranen-ot-kmetstvoto-news9794.html)
- [ALLEGATION, prosecution] Gurko 1 business centre: failure to carry out the council's decision of 14 July 1997 on an in-kind contribution, alleged damage about 1.035 billion old leva; and sale of the municipality's 34% stake to NIKMI for 2,673,840 leva instead of the agreed 3,191,357.30 leva (shortfall 517,517.30 leva). — [Mediapool](https://www.mediapool.bg/s-treto-obvinenie-sofiyanski-mozhe-zavinagi-da-bade-otstranen-ot-kmetstvoto-news9794.html)
- [SELF-CLAIM] His defence: the deals were approved by the municipal council and he was executing collective decisions. — [Mediapool](https://www.mediapool.bg/s-treto-obvinenie-sofiyanski-mozhe-zavinagi-da-bade-otstranen-ot-kmetstvoto-news9794.html)
- [FACT] Chief Prosecutor Nikola Filchev asked parliament to lift his immunity on 27 September and 25 October 2005; parliament voted to lift it on 22 February 2006 (he asked colleagues to vote in favour). — [bg.wikipedia](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Стефан_Софиянски)
- [FACT] June 2006: acquitted by Sofia City Court in the Central Halls case. September 2007: acquitted in the Millennium Center case. February 2008: acquitted in the Gurko/NIKMI case after the prosecutor himself said the evidence was insufficient. — [bg.wikipedia](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Стефан_Софиянски), citing [Dnevnik, 19 Feb 2008](http://www.dnevnik.bg/bulgaria/2008/02/19/460794_sofiianski_az_sum_edinstveniiat_dokazano_nekorumpiran/) and [vesti.bg](http://www.vesti.bg/novini/bivshiiat-stolichen-kmet-sledval-reshenieto-na-obshtinskiia-syvet-i-niamalo-kak-da-byde-osyden-za-tova-che-e-izpylniaval-kolektiva-volia-901628)
- [SELF-CLAIM] He said Filchev brought the cases "to order" for a circle around him, and called himself "the only proven non-corrupt" politician. — [bg.wikipedia](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Стефан_Софиянски); [Dnevnik headline](http://www.dnevnik.bg/bulgaria/2008/02/19/460794_sofiianski_az_sum_edinstveniiat_dokazano_nekorumpiran/)
- [REPORTED] The Green Party filed a complaint against him for criminal inaction over waste (outcome not stated). — [paragraf.bg](https://paragraf.bg/stefan-sofiyanski-otteglih-se-kato-kmet-zaradi-krizata-s-bokluka)

**Sofiyski imoti (municipal company)**
- [REPORTED] Founded in late 1995 as a 100% municipal company; first board chair Simeon Simeonov (BSP councillor); executive director Toshko Dobrev (Democratic Party councillor, described as close to council chair Antoan Nikolov). — [Mediapool, 13 March 2017](https://www.mediapool.bg/megadeloto-sofiyski-imoti-priklyuchi-toshko-dobrev-e-nevinen-news261264.html)
- [FACT per State Financial Inspection, as reported] From April 1998 to 27 November 2003 the company made nearly 1,000 deals with municipal property; a 2003 State Financial Inspection (ДВФК) report concluded this amounted to de facto "hidden privatisation" of municipal property. — [Mediapool](https://www.mediapool.bg/megadeloto-sofiyski-imoti-priklyuchi-toshko-dobrev-e-nevinen-news261264.html)
- [REPORTED] Deals that never reached court: 54 decares in South Park valued at 19 million leva (early-2003 prices) contributed to Sofia City Company AD; 242,000 sq m contributed to PFC Slavia 1913 AD at 14.2 leva/sq m against a triple expert valuation of EUR 29/sq m; nearly 56 decares of Borisova gradina contributed to Park Maria Luiza AD at 37 leva/sq m. — [Mediapool](https://www.mediapool.bg/megadeloto-sofiyski-imoti-priklyuchi-toshko-dobrev-e-nevinen-news261264.html)
- [REPORTED, snippet only] 2002–2003: 35 properties and flats transferred from Sofiyski imoti to Zavodproekt in exchange for 3 floors of an office building at 12 Gurko St. — [Capital 2011](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2011/06/10/1104209_sofiiski_gradski_imoti/)
- [FACT] The prosecution began examining the company in 2006 (after his term) on a report by the Capital Inspectorate; the prosecution's theory across several dozen cases was an identical scheme of deals at understated prices. — [Mediapool](https://www.mediapool.bg/megadeloto-sofiyski-imoti-priklyuchi-toshko-dobrev-e-nevinen-news261264.html); [Capital 2011, snippet only](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2011/06/10/1104209_sofiiski_gradski_imoti/)
- [FACT] Outcomes for Dobrev: 4-year sentence in January 2012 (Simetra 1 case, damage about 342,000 leva), terminated by the Supreme Court of Cassation on 17 September 2015 for limitation; February 2012 conviction over a 1998 swap of land in Lyulin 10 for 26 Peugeot cars (damage 116,000 leva per prosecution); later acquittals, the last becoming final in February 2017 (Astrima shares, 126,000 leva sale, 40,000 leva alleged damage). He later won compensation from the prosecution. — [Mediapool 2017](https://www.mediapool.bg/megadeloto-sofiyski-imoti-priklyuchi-toshko-dobrev-e-nevinen-news261264.html); [BNR](https://old-news.bnr.bg/sofia/post/100243870/4-godini-zatvor-za-bivshiyat-izpylnitelen-direktor-nasofiiski-imoti-toshko-dobrev); [24chasa](https://www.24chasa.bg/bulgaria/article/13596234); [lex.bg](https://news.lex.bg/обвиненията-за-аферата-софийски-имо/)
- [SELF-CLAIM, Dobrev] The decisions on the deals belonged to the municipal council. — search summary of [Frognews](https://mobile.frognews.bg/article/42955/)

### Inferences
- Court record for Sofiyanski himself: three charges, three first-instance acquittals, zero convictions. The record does not show corruption proven against him.
- The institutional finding that stands regardless of the acquittals is the State Financial Inspection's "hidden privatisation" conclusion about Sofiyski imoti, a company wholly owned by the municipality and operating throughout his tenure. Responsibility is shared with the municipal council (which approved deals) and cross-party (BSP board chair, Democratic Party director).
- Low conviction rates here reflect both weak prosecutions and limitation periods, so acquittals are not strong evidence that the deals were good value for the municipality; equally, the valuations quoted are prosecution/press figures.

### Gaps
- Whether the three acquittals of Sofiyanski were appealed and when they became final was not confirmed (Dnevnik page 403).
- Sofiyanski was never, as far as I found, a defendant in the Sofiyski imoti cases; I found no source stating he was charged over the swaps ("заменки"). The term "заменки" is more commonly tied to state forest/land swaps of 2005–2009; its application to his period rests on the Sofiyski imoti swaps above.
- Total number of properties moved into Sofiyski imoti and an overall damage estimate were not found in a readable source.
- The 2003 State Financial Inspection report itself was not located; it is cited via Mediapool.

## 4. Sofiyska voda water concession (1999/2000)

### Takeaway
The 25-year concession was signed on 23 December 1999 and took effect on 6 October 2000, with no concession fee to the city. The National Audit Office found in 2005 that for four years the mayor submitted no annual report to the council on the concessionaire's performance, a breach of the Municipal Property Act and the city's own ordinance; the Audit Office's president nevertheless judged the contract itself "normal", not clearly disadvantageous.

### Cited Findings
- [FACT] Contract signed 23 December 1999, in force 6 October 2000, 25-year term; no concession fee is provided for in the contract. — Sofia Municipality concession register, as quoted in search results — [sofia.bg, Концесия №5](https://www.sofia.bg/en/w/koncesia-001)
- [FACT] Concessionaire Sofiyska Voda was owned by International Water Ltd (Bechtel, Edison) and United Utilities, with the municipality as minority shareholder (about 25% at the start; 22.9% by 2010 when Veolia bought 77.1%). — [Bankwatch](https://bankwatch.org/?p=111927); [Social Watch 2003](https://www.socialwatch.org/sites/default/files/pdf/en/bulgaria2003_eng.pdf)
- [FACT] EBRD signed a EUR 31 million loan to Sofiyska Voda in December 2000. — [Bankwatch](https://bankwatch.org/?p=111927)
- [FACT, audit finding] National Audit Office (audited period about four years, report published October 2005): the mayor did not submit a single report to the municipal council on how the concessionaire met its obligations, and the council never asked for one; this breached the Municipal Property Act and the municipal ordinance requiring annual reports. Monitoring company Omonit did prepare periodic reports, but there is no evidence the council ever considered them. — [Prof. Valeri Dimitrov, Audit Office president, Dnevnik 11 Oct 2005, on bulnao.government.bg](https://bulnao.government.bg/bg/documents/12003/interview-dnevnik111005.doc)
- [FACT, audit position] Asked if both mayor and council were at fault: "Так излиза" ("So it appears"). The audit team could not find data on whether the concessionaire fulfilled its investment obligations, because the municipality had not exercised its control rights. — [same](https://bulnao.government.bg/bg/documents/12003/interview-dnevnik111005.doc)
- [OPINION of the Audit Office president] "I would not say the contract was badly concluded"; it includes a commitment of 150 million in investment over 25 years and agreed service levels; the open question is why no concession fee was agreed, although the law at the time did not explicitly require one. Recommendation: the mayor must report annually. — [same](https://bulnao.government.bg/bg/documents/12003/interview-dnevnik111005.doc)
- [FACT, later documents] The contract guaranteed the concessionaire a 17% return on equity (cut to 13% when the concession was extended to February 2034). — [Mediapool](https://www.mediapool.bg/kontsesiyata-na-sofiyska-voda-shte-bade-udalzhena-do-2034-g-news342132.html); [Sofia Municipal Council annex](https://council.sofia.bg/documents/d/guest/r-898_pr-1)
- [REPORTED] July 2002: the municipality itself accused International Water of contract breaches and poor service; Raiffeisen Investment had warned in November 1999 that the investor might struggle to invest. — [Social Watch 2003](https://www.socialwatch.org/sites/default/files/pdf/en/bulgaria2003_eng.pdf) (advocacy source)
- [ALLEGATION] Of EUR 15 million of EBRD money disbursed on 22 December 2000, EUR 13.5 million was transferred abroad to the shareholders. — [Social Watch 2003](https://www.socialwatch.org/sites/default/files/pdf/en/bulgaria2003_eng.pdf) (advocacy source, media-based)
- [ALLEGATION, political] Water price rose more than 300% since 2000 and losses/uncollected exceeded 60% (claim by VMRO's Angel Dzhambazki in 2011). — search summary of [Capital 2011](https://www.capital.bg/biznes/kompanii/2011/10/14/1177026_do_posledna_kapka_voda/) (snippet only)
- [FACT] Network water losses were 58% in 2009, nine years into the concession. — [Bankwatch, citing the energy and water regulator](https://bankwatch.org/?p=111927)

### Inferences
- The clearest documented failing of his administration in this file is supervisory: four years without the legally required reporting on a 25-year monopoly contract. This is an official audit finding, not an allegation.
- The contract terms (no fee, 17% guaranteed return) were later treated as unfavourable enough to be renegotiated, but the auditors of the time did not call the contract itself disadvantageous.

### Gaps
- The Audit Office report itself (number, exact audited period, full findings) was not located, only the president's interview.
- Tariff path 2000–2005 and investment actually made by 2005 were not found in a primary source.

## 5. Waste: cleaning concessions and the Suhodol crisis

### Takeaway
Sofia had a single landfill (Suhodol) and no replacement was built during ten years in office; promises to close it by end-2004, then mid-2005, were missed, producing blockades in January and July 2005, a declared epidemic-risk crisis and years of costly waste baling. Street cleaning was run under long concessions held by three firms linked to Rumen Gaytanski, which had received over 300 million leva by 2006.

### Cited Findings
- [REPORTED] In 2001 the mayor promised to close Suhodol (about 500 m from the neighbourhood) by the end of 2004; the deadline was missed. — [Global Nonviolent Action Database, Swarthmore](https://nvdatabase.swarthmore.edu/content/bulgarian-residents-blockade-landfill-suhodol-neighborhood-sofia-2005) (compiled from AFP, AP, SeeNews 2005)
- [REPORTED] 7–14 January 2005: residents blocked the landfill; about 200 trucks with 4,000 tonnes were held up; the council held an emergency session; agreement that only hard waste would go to Suhodol after June and closure by September 2005. — [Swarthmore database](https://nvdatabase.swarthmore.edu/content/bulgarian-residents-blockade-landfill-suhodol-neighborhood-sofia-2005); [Novinite](https://www.novinite.com/articles/43530/Agreement+Reached+on+Sofia+Garbage+Dump)
- [REPORTED] Spring 2005: replacement/baling sites met protests from other neighbourhoods; 30 June 2005 deadline for a new storage facility was missed (the day after he left office). — [Swarthmore database](https://nvdatabase.swarthmore.edu/content/bulgarian-residents-blockade-landfill-suhodol-neighborhood-sofia-2005)
- [REPORTED] Early July 2005 (under acting mayor Gerdzhikov): new blockade; 5 July epidemic-crisis declared (hepatitis A, salmonella fears at about 30°C); 8 July riot police dispersed the blockade, about 100 trucks got through, two officers lightly injured. — [Swarthmore database](https://nvdatabase.swarthmore.edu/content/bulgarian-residents-blockade-landfill-suhodol-neighborhood-sofia-2005); [RFE/RL Bulgaria](https://bulgaria.rferl.org/a/sofiyanski-borisov-gaytanski-valka-krizi-bokluk-sofiya/33561569.html)
- [FACT] Suhodol had operated since the 1970s; its permit ran to the end of 2005; it closed in October 2005 and was reopened by government decision in autumn 2007 for 18 months. — [RFE/RL Bulgaria](https://bulgaria.rferl.org/a/sofiyanski-borisov-gaytanski-valka-krizi-bokluk-sofiya/33561569.html); [Novinite](https://www.novinite.com/articles/87529/Bulgaria+Environment+Min+OKs+Re-opening+Suhodol+Dumpsite)
- [REPORTED] November 2005: cabinet approved temporary storage of bales at the Chukurovo mine; clashes left 12 injured; the decision was later declared unlawful. — [RFE/RL Bulgaria](https://bulgaria.rferl.org/a/sofiyanski-borisov-gaytanski-valka-krizi-bokluk-sofiya/33561569.html)
- [REPORTED, snippet only] Legacy: Borisov inherited a closed landfill and several baling sites; about 280,000 bales were later moved to Karlovo and Tsalapitsa; baling is "claimed" to have cost over 80 million leva (no exact figure known). Deputy mayor for ecology Milor Mihaylov resigned over the crisis. — [Capital 2008](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2008/08/29/543846_kriza_s_bokluka_na_sofiia_otnovo/)
- [SELF-CLAIM] 2005: "We sit in the middle of a conflict that is very clear" (everyone wants waste removed, nobody wants it near them). 2026: the landfill was legal; protesters included NDSV and BSP party members. — [RFE/RL](https://bulgaria.rferl.org/a/sofiyanski-borisov-gaytanski-valka-krizi-bokluk-sofiya/33561569.html); [paragraf.bg](https://paragraf.bg/stefan-sofiyanski-otteglih-se-kato-kmet-zaradi-krizata-s-bokluka)
- [OPINION, successor] Borisov called his predecessor's inaction on waste "criminal". — [RFE/RL](https://bulgaria.rferl.org/a/sofiyanski-borisov-gaytanski-valka-krizi-bokluk-sofiya/33561569.html)
- **Cleaning concessions.** [REPORTED, snippet only] Contract with Volf 96 signed 1997 (expiring 2009); contracts with Chistota Sofia and DITZ signed 1999 (expiring 2014); from signing to mid-2006 the three firms were paid over 300 million leva; Rumen Gaytanski officially held 33% in two of them. — [Capital, 9 June 2006](https://www.capital.bg/biznes/kompanii/2006/06/09/266340_otivat_li_si_mutrite_ot_biznesa_s_bokluka/)
- [REPORTED] Gaytanski ("Вълка") held Sofia's waste collection since the 1990s, described as a practical monopoly; the firms were sold to Equest/Novera in 2007–2008. — [RFE/RL](https://bulgaria.rferl.org/a/sofiyanski-borisov-gaytanski-valka-krizi-bokluk-sofiya/33561569.html); [Capital 2008, snippet only](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2008/03/14/471097_ot_mrusna_po-mrusna/)
- [SELF-CLAIM] Gaytanski was "inherited from the previous mayor"; cleaning cost 65 million leva of a 400 million budget. — [paragraf.bg](https://paragraf.bg/stefan-sofiyanski-otteglih-se-kato-kmet-zaradi-krizata-s-bokluka)
- [ALLEGATION, successor] Borisov claimed the concessions were structured to give disproportionate profits. — [Capital 2006, snippet only](https://www.capital.bg/biznes/kompanii/2006/06/09/266340_otivat_li_si_mutrite_ot_biznesa_s_bokluka/)

### Inferences
- The waste failure is a planning failure sustained over the whole tenure: the landfill's limits were known (he himself set a 2004 closure date in 2001), yet no permitted replacement or treatment plant existed when the deadline arrived. The acute July 2005 phase occurred days after he left, but flowed directly from missed deadlines set under him.
- His "inherited from the previous mayor" claim sits awkwardly with the reported signing dates (1997 and 1999, both within his tenure), though the firms may have worked for the city earlier under other arrangements.
- If his own figures are right, cleaning took about 16% of the budget (65/400 million leva): an unverified self-reported ratio.

### Gaps
- The concession contracts' terms (duration, pricing formula, penalty clauses) and the council decisions awarding them were not read in a primary source; the dates rest on one Capital snippet.
- No audit (Audit Office or financial inspection) of the cleaning concessions for this period was found.
- No reliable total for baling costs; the "over 80 million leva" figure is explicitly a claim.
