# Sofia Municipal Council (СОС): composition and record, 1995–2026

Research date: 14 November 2026. Access notes that affect confidence:

- `results.cik.bg`, `capital.bg` and `web.archive.org` could not be opened (HTTP 403 / blocked). CIK figures are therefore taken from media reports of the Municipal Election Commission (ОИК) announcements and from Wikipedia tables that cite CIK. Capital articles are cited only for what their headline or search snippet shows, and are marked "(snippet)".
- For 1995–2011 the strongest source is the council's own archived member lists (PDFs on council.sofia.bg). I downloaded and counted them myself; the seat counts for 1995 and 1999 are my counts from those lists, not published totals.

## 1. Seat distribution, vote share and turnout for each of the eight elections

### Takeaway
A seat distribution summing to 61 was found for all eight terms. 2003–2023 are confirmed by at least two sources; 1995 and 1999 rest on a single primary source (the council's own member lists) and should be treated as medium confidence, especially on whether the 1999 "Radicals" group was a separate list or a split.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- GERB's seat count fell at every election it contested: 33 (2007), 33 (2011), 30 (2015), 27 (2019), 14 (2023). GERB held an outright majority only in 2007–2015.
- 1995: "ОДО" is the label used in the council's list for the SDS-led list; reading entries marked "от [month, year]" as replacements is my interpretation. It is the only reading that produces exactly 61.
- 1999: several members of the "Радикали" group (e.g. Krasimir Arsov, Georgi Tsonkov) sat as ОДО councillors in 1995–1999, so the group may have been either a separate pro-Sofiyanski list or a breakaway from ОДС. The implied 27 SDS seats in the English Wikipedia 2003 table supports the reading that ОДС won 27 at the election, but that table is itself unsourced for 1999.
- The Mediapool "34" for GERB in 2007–2011 may reflect a councillor joining the group mid-term; no source confirms this.

### Gaps
- Official CIK vote counts and turnout for the Sofia council lists for every election (results.cik.bg blocked). Sofia-specific turnout was found only for 1999 (51.6%) and approximately for 2019.
- 1995 and 1999: no election-night seat table, vote shares or second independent source. The IME library bulletin for 1999 (http://library.ime.bg/byuletin-za-rezultatite-ot-mestnite-izbori-proizvedeni-na-16-i-23-oktomvri-1999g-/) is cited by Bulgarian Wikipedia and would settle 1999; it was not opened.
- Whether БДС "Радикали" ran its own list in 1999.

## 2. Council chairs and working majorities, by term

### Takeaway
Chairs came from the SDS family in 1995–2007, from GERB in 2007–2023, and from PP–DB since February 2024. The mayor had a same-party outright majority only in 2007–2015; in 2015–2023 GERB governed with small partners, and since 2023 Mayor Terziev's list has been the largest group without controlling the council.

### Cited Findings

**Chairs**
- Petar Stankov chaired 1991–1995; Evgeniy Bakardzhiev 1995–1997; Antoan Nikolov 1997–2003; Vladimir Kisyov 2003–2007; Andrey Ivanov 2007–2012; Elen Gerdzhikov 2012–2021; Georgi Georgiev 2021–2023; Tsvetomir Petrov from 8 February 2024 — [bg.wikipedia, Столичен общински съвет](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Столичен_общински_съвет)
- Bakardzhiev (ОДО) was chair until March 1997; Antoan Nikolov (ОДО), who entered the council in December 1995 as a replacement, was chair from March 1997 — [council.sofia.bg, list 1995–1999](https://council.sofia.bg/documents/20182/21367/%D0%9E%D0%B1%D1%89%D0%B8%D0%BD%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8+%D1%81%D1%8A%D0%B2%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B8+%D0%9C%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%82+1995+-+1999+%D0%B3/c8933b82-7a65-4230-b80f-4e0d6c7a43cf)
- Nikolov was both council chair and head of the ОДС group in 1999–2003 — [council.sofia.bg, list 1999–2003](https://council.sofia.bg/documents/20182/21367/%D0%9E%D0%B1%D1%89%D0%B8%D0%BD%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8+%D1%81%D1%8A%D0%B2%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B8+%D0%9C%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%82+1999+-+2003+%D0%B3/2182ccf5-654c-4137-a178-3545e19b5cd5)
- Kisyov (СДС) chaired 2003–2007; vice-chairs came from BSP (Micho Raykovski), SDS (Borislav Borislavov), SSD (Milan Milanov) and Gergyovden (Stoyan Chakarov) — [council.sofia.bg, list 2003–2007](https://council.sofia.bg/documents/20182/21367/%D0%9E%D0%B1%D1%89%D0%B8%D0%BD%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8+%D1%81%D1%8A%D0%B2%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B8+%D0%9C%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%82+2003+-+2007+%D0%B3/9d09799a-fd58-4d6f-b656-322683d703eb)
- Andrey Ivanov (ГЕРБ) chaired 2007–2011 — [council.sofia.bg, list 2007–2011](https://council.sofia.bg/documents/20182/21367/%D0%9E%D0%B1%D1%89%D0%B8%D0%BD%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8+%D1%81%D1%8A%D0%B2%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B8+%D0%9C%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%82+2007+-+2011+%D0%B3/8c945271-12c2-4464-86cc-05746845b3ce)
- Ivanov was re-elected chair on 8 November (2011) by secret vote, defeating BSP's Georgi Kadiev, then resigned on 11 January 2012; GERB nominated Elen Gerdzhikov, with the vote set for 12 January 2012 and 31 votes needed (GERB had 33) — [Novinite, 12 Jan 2012](https://www.novinite.com/articles/135637/Sofia+Municipal+Council+Appoints+New+Head)

### Inferences
- Mayor with a secure majority: 1995–1999 (bare), 1999–2003 (comfortable, if the Radicals are counted with ОДС), 2007–2015 (outright GERB). Mayor dependent on partners: 2003–2007, 2015–2019, 2019–2023. Mayor in a minority facing an organised counter-majority: 2023–present.
- 2005–2007: Boyko Borissov became mayor in 2005 with no party group of his own in the 2003 council (GERB did not yet exist as a council list), so he governed with a council elected under Sofiyanski.
- The chair change of January 2012 was within GERB and did not change the majority.

### Gaps
- Exact date and vote of Gerdzhikov's election in January 2012, and the exact date of his 2021 resignation.
- Vote tally for Petrov on 8 February 2024, and whether his "temporary" mandate was formally renewed.
- Exact dates and votes for the elections of Bakardzhiev (1995), Nikolov (1997, 1999), Kisyov (2003) and Ivanov (2007).

## 4. Key decisions and their consequences, by term

### Takeaway
Hard evidence was found for the Sofiyska voda concession (1999–2000), the 2005–2007 waste crisis, the 2016 fare rise, budget votes in 2020–2026 and the July 2024 replacement of six municipal company boards. Council votes on property swaps, the General Urban Plan, parking zones, tax and waste-fee rates and individual loans were not found and are listed as gaps.

### Cited Findings

**1995–1999 and 1999–2003 (Sofiyanski)**
- The concession contract between Sofia Municipality and "Софийска вода" АД was signed on 23 December 1999 and took effect on 6 October 2000; it has been amended several times, including in 2018 — [council.sofia.bg, Decision 898 (2022)](https://council.sofia.bg/documents/d/guest/r-898)
- The concession runs 25 years and expires on 6 October 2025 (snippet) — [Capital, 21 Nov 2022](https://www.capital.bg/biznes/kompanii/2022/11/21/4418195_koncesiiata_na_vik_mrejata_na_sofiia_shte_bude/)

### Inferences
- Budget timing worsened over the last three terms: early February (2020), end of March (2022, 2024), late September (2026). The 2026 delay is attributed to the state budget rather than the council.
- The 2024 budget passed with a broad cross-party vote (45–15) even though the same council was split 31–29 on company boards four months later, so the "counter-majority" operates on appointments and property more than on the budget.
- The Sofiyska voda concession's stated expiry (October 2025) falls inside the current term; what the council decided about it is a material open question for rating both the 1999 and 2023 councils.

## 5. Documented scandals involving councillors

### Takeaway
The best-documented case is "Софийски имоти": councillors of the 1999–2003 and 2003–2007 councils sat on the board of a municipal property company and were charged in 2008 over about 2 million leva of below-market sales. Chair Andrey Ivanov resigned in 2012 under property allegations, and Chair Gerdzhikov was pushed out in 2021 by his own party leader. No conviction of a sitting councillor was verified.

### Inferences
- The Minchev and Stancheva charged in 2008 are very probably the 1999–2003 ОДС councillors of the same names, which would mean councillors from two consecutive councils sat on the "Софийски имоти" board during the period of the disputed sales. The match is by name only.
- Final outcomes of the "Софийски имоти" prosecutions are unclear: convictions at first instance, then at least one acquittal, with no verified final judgment.

### Gaps
- No verified information on any charge, arrest or conviction of Vladimir Kisyov; two searches returned nothing.
- Names of councillors who personally benefited from property swaps, and any prosecution over swap votes.
- Whether the property allegations against Andrey Ivanov led to any proceedings.
- Final court outcomes for Dobrev, Zashev and the other "Софийски имоти" defendants.
- The reported finding that "Софийски имоти" went from over 1,100 properties to 257 between 1998 and 2003 appeared only in a search summary without a clear source page; treat as unverified.
- Declared conflicts of interest for councillors in any term.

## 6. Attendance, transparency, voting data and board fees

### Takeaway
Very little systematic data was found. The only concrete points are a 2012 Capital report on the legal cap on councillor pay and the ban on board seats (with a Sofia loophole for audit committees), and the council's own count of decisions.

### Cited Findings
- As of 2012, councillor pay was capped at no more than 60% of the average salary in the municipal administration, and sitting on boards of municipal companies was banned; in Sofia about 10–15 of the 61 councillors could still sit on companies' audit committees (snippet) — [Capital, 13 Jul 2012](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2012/07/13/1864806_bezplatni_suveti/)
- Before that ban, councillors' votes decided concessions, tenders, swaps of municipal land and procurement in the country's richest municipality, and Borissov often left council sittings in his first years as mayor (snippet) — [Capital, 21 Sep 2007](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2007/09/21/379035_suvetnici_na_kilogram/)
- Councillors sat on the board of "Софийски имоти" in 2003–2004 — [Mediapool, 3 Dec 2008](https://www.mediapool.bg/osem-shefove-na-sofiyski-imoti-obvineni-za-2-mln-lv-shteti-news146623.html)
- The chair election is by secret ballot, so individual votes for chair are not recorded (2012, 2021, 2024) — [Novinite, 2012](https://www.novinite.com/articles/135637/Sofia+Municipal+Council+Appoints+New+Head); [RFE/RL Bulgaria, 9 Feb 2024](https://bulgaria.rferl.org/a/sos-mnozinstvo-sofia/32811845.html)
- 566 decisions were adopted in the first year of the 2023–2027 term — [BTA video](https://www.bta.bg/bg/videos/23887)
- Capital published pieces on transparency in the council (2019) and on interests in the council (2024); neither could be opened — [Capital, 15 Nov 2019](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/2019/11/15/3991863_poveche_prozrachnost_v_obshtinskiia_suvet/); [Capital, 17 Sep 2024](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/gradove/2024/09/17/4676812_interesi_v_tumen_sos/1726744945b8de874bbacbb8ff6218120ae75d4e55)
- Session minutes and decisions are published on council.sofia.bg, including archives back to 2003–2007 — [council.sofia.bg, previous mandates](https://council.sofia.bg/en/previous-mandates)

### Inferences
- Because board membership for councillors was banned by 2012, board-fee scandals belong mainly to the 1995–2011 councils; after that the issue shifts to who the council majority appoints to boards (2024, 2026).

### Gaps
- No NGO or media dataset on councillor attendance or roll-call voting was found for any term.
- No figures for board fees paid to councillors in any term.
- The year the board-seat ban took effect, and whether the audit-committee exception still applies.
- Content of the two Capital transparency articles.
