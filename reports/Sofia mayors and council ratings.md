# Sofia built a metro but neglected oversight

Across thirty years no mayor of Sofia and no Sofia Municipal Council (Столичен общински съвет, СОС) earns a high mark on the evidence found: on this report's 1–10 scale the mayors score **Fandakova 5.5, Terziev 5.2 (provisional), Borisov 4.8 and Sofiyanski 3.8**, and the eight councils range from **3.0 (2003–2007) to 5.8 (2011–2015)**. These numbers are this report's own judgement, built from a stated rubric and the cited evidence; they are not an official measure, and a reasonable reader could move any of them by a point. The pattern behind them is consistent: the city delivered hard infrastructure, above all a metro that grew from nothing in 1996 to **50 stations and 55 km** in 2026, while repeatedly failing on waste, on control of municipal property and companies, and on planning. The National Audit Office reached the same structural finding under two different mayors: property was sold or swapped with no strategy and almost no competition (only **2.13%** of sales in 1998–2005 went by auction or tender; **140 swaps** in 2006–March 2008 were done without auctions). Criminal cases against mayors and their deputies mostly ended in acquittal (Sofiyanski three times, deputy mayor Krusev at three instances) or are unresolved (Barbutov), so the firmest negative evidence is audit findings and court rulings against the municipality, not convictions. Councils with a secure single-party majority (2007–2015) functioned smoothly but supervised weakly; fragmented councils (2003–2007, 2023–) supervised no better and functioned worse. Large gaps remain: official election commission vote counts could not be retrieved, municipal debt before 2010 was not found, and no perception survey yet covers the Terziev term.

## How the ratings were built: six criteria for mayors, four for councils

**Status of the ratings.** Every score below is the report's judgement drawn from the evidence in the research notes. No Bulgarian institution rates mayors or councils this way. Scores are whole numbers per criterion; the overall mark is the unweighted average, shown to one decimal. Where evidence for a criterion is thin, the score is pulled towards the middle and marked "low confidence". Terziev and the 2023–2027 council are rated on three of four years and are **provisional**.

**Scale.** 1–2: serious documented failure with lasting cost; 3–4: more documented failure than delivery; 5–6: mixed record; 7–8: clear documented delivery with limited failures; 9–10: sustained delivery confirmed by independent sources, with no serious adverse finding. Nobody reaches 9.

**Mayor criteria (equal weight).** (1) Infrastructure and transport delivery; (2) waste and environment, including air; (3) finances and debt; (4) integrity, legality and audit findings; (5) urban planning; (6) basic services (public transport operation, kindergartens, water, heating, cleaning, stray animals).

**Council criteria (equal weight).** (1) Oversight of the mayor and municipal companies; (2) quality of major decisions; (3) integrity; (4) stability and functioning.

**Attribution rules.** Three corrections are applied throughout. Inherited problems are not charged to the successor: Borisov inherited a closed landfill and cleaning concessions signed in 1998–1999; Terziev inherited a district heating company with over BGN 1.5 bn of liabilities at end-2023 ([S&P, Feb 2024](https://www.sofia.bg/documents/d/guest/2024-02-19-research-update_city-of-sofia_feb-16-2024)). State projects are not credited or charged to the mayor: the Northern Speed Tangent was contracted by the Road Infrastructure Agency and financed under a state-run operational programme ([Mediapool, 28 Apr 2016](https://www.mediapool.bg/severnata-tangenta-na-sofiya-pusnata-s-dva-mesetsa-po-rano-news248671.html)); the 2017 EU Court judgment on air quality was against Bulgaria, not the municipality ([SeeNews](https://seenews.com/news/eu-court-of-justice-rules-against-bulgaria-on-air-quality-564210)); the General Urban Plan was adopted by Parliament as a law ([National Assembly](https://parliament.bg/bills/43/602-01-67.pdf)). Structural advantages are noted: EU grants were not available before 2007, and Sofia's credit rating is capped at the sovereign's, so upgrades track Bulgaria's rating more than any mayor's decisions ([S&P, Feb 2024](https://www.sofia.bg/documents/d/guest/2024-02-19-research-update_city-of-sofia_feb-16-2024)).

**Evidence labels used in the tables.** **D** = documented (court ruling, Audit Office report, official statistic, council decision, signed contract, operator's own records). **R** = reported by the press as fact, not checked against a primary document. **S** = self-reported by the municipality or the mayor. **A** = allegation, charge, or claim by an interested party. **N** = seen only in a search snippet or summary; the page itself was not read. Acquittals are stated wherever charges are mentioned.

## Sofiyanski (1995–2005): a metro opened, a landfill left to fail — 3.8

Stefan Sofiyanski governed for nine years and seven months, winning in 1995 (runoff), 1999 (first round, about 69%) and 2003 (runoff, about 54%, as leader of his own party after breaking with SDS); these percentages come from secondary sources, not the election commission ([bg.wikipedia](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Стефан_Софиянски)). A court suspended him from office from 5 May to 29 July 2004 at the prosecution's request, and he left on 29 June 2005 for a seat in Parliament, in the middle of the Suhodol waste crisis and with three criminal cases pending ([Mediapool, 2 Jul 2004](https://www.mediapool.bg/s-treto-obvinenie-sofiyanski-mozhe-zavinagi-da-bade-otstranen-ot-kmetstvoto-news9794.html)). He was **acquitted in all three cases** (June 2006, September 2007, February 2008); no conviction was found ([bg.wikipedia](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/Стефан_Софиянски)).

**Sub-scores and why.** *Infrastructure 6:* the metro went from zero to **8 stations and about 10 km**, and the municipality raised market finance two years after hyperinflation; but much of the tunnelling predated 1995 and no source quantifies his share, and evidence on roads is descriptive only. *Waste 2:* the city had one landfill, his own 2001 promise to close it by end-2004 was missed, no replacement existed, and the cleaning concessions carried no fee and paid on inputs, not waste removed. *Finances 5:* the 1999 Eurobond was redeemed in June 2002 mostly from the budget, but in 2000 his own finance deputy warned of "financial collapse", and no debt series exists for the period (low confidence). *Integrity 3:* three acquittals mean no corruption was proven against him personally, yet two Audit Office reports and a state financial-control audit found property and concessions managed without competition, fee or supervision. *Planning 3:* dense building under piecemeal amendments to the old plan is press-reported, not measured (low confidence). *Services 4:* the first EU perception wave (2006), a baseline for what he left, shows **23.4%** satisfied with public transport and **25.4%** with green spaces ([Eurostat urb_percep](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/api/dissemination/statistics/1.0/data/urb_percep?format=JSON&lang=EN&cities=BG001C)).

### Acting mayor Minko Gerdzhikov (2005 and 2009) — not rated

Deputy mayor Gerdzhikov acted as mayor from 30 June 2005 until Borisov took office (sources give 9 or 12 November 2005) and again from late July to 23 November 2009 ([en.wikipedia, List of mayors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Sofia)). In the first spell he, not Sofiyanski, declared the crisis over the July 2005 Suhodol blockade ([RFE/RL](https://bulgaria.rferl.org/a/sofiyanski-borisov-gaytanski-valka-krizi-bokluk-sofiya/33561569.html)). In the second, the Serdika–Vasil Levski Stadium metro link opened on 7–8 September 2009 and he signed a **EUR 43.5 m** EIB loan for road junctions on 22 October 2009 ([EIB](https://www.eib.org/en/press/all/2009-208-eib-boosts-infrastructure-and-small-companies-in-bulgaria)). He later resigned as deputy mayor for finance in March 2011 over a property-tax scandal ([Novinite](https://www.novinite.com/articles/126167/Sofia+Deputy+Mayor+Resigns+over+Property+Tax+Scandal)). Two caretaker periods of about four months each are too short to rate.

## Borisov (2005–2009): metro contracts signed, waste promises missed — 4.8

Boyko Borisov won a partial election in 2005 as an independent (runoff about 68%, turnout about 28%) and was re-elected in the first round on 28 October 2007 with 53.43% (202,800 votes) as GERB's candidate; he left in late July 2009 to become prime minister ([Novinite, 5 Nov 2005](https://www.novinite.com/articles/54770/Exit+Polls%3A+Independent+Borissov+Elected+Sofia+Mayor+with+68); [Novinite, 29 Oct 2007](https://www.novinite.com/articles/86963/Sofia+Mayor+Secures+Re-election+with+53.43+-+Final+Results)). For his first two years he worked with a council elected under Sofiyanski in which he had no group.

**Sub-scores and why.** *Infrastructure 7:* six stations and about 8 km opened in 2009, Line 2 contracts were signed and the EIB lent directly to a Bulgarian municipality for the first time; the central section ran about two years late, and the project and its Japanese loan were inherited. *Waste 3:* the inherited part is not charged, but on his watch no plant was started despite repeated deadlines, bale stockpiles grew, the old landfill was reopened, the European Commission opened and escalated an infringement, and the city went uncleaned in March 2009. *Finances 6:* S&P raised the outlook in February 2006 citing better budget performance; no debt figure exists for 2005–2009 (low confidence). *Integrity 4:* he removed the heating company's director within five months and says he ended swaps, but the Audit Office found 140 swaps without strategy or auction on his watch and his transport deputy left over a recorded bid-rigging meeting. *Planning 4:* a chief architect was appointed by competition and an unlawful permit was stopped, but the plan that took effect in 2007 carried the Ombudsman's objections on green areas. *Services 5:* satisfaction with public transport rose from 23.4% (2006) to 42.8% (2009), while about 5,000 children were left without a kindergarten place in 2008.

## Fandakova (2009–2023): 33 metro stations, then stagnating control — 5.5

Yordanka Fandakova (GERB) won in 2009 (66.23% on 23.17% turnout), in the first round in 2011 and 2015, and only in a runoff in 2019 with **49.98%** (209,542 votes) against 45.13% ([en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yordanka_Fandakova); [News.bg, 4 Nov 2019](https://news.bg/politics/okonchatelno-49-98-za-fandakova-45-13-za-manolova.html)). Fourteen years give her both the largest delivery and the longest list of audit findings. Her period also had what no predecessor had: EU grants. Metropolitan's own pages name about **EUR 805 m** of grants for sections opened in her term (the report's sum of 157 + 200 + 110 + 338) ([Metropolitan, stage 1](https://www.metropolitan.bg/razshiryavane/istoriya-razvitie-metro/razshirenie-etap-1); [stage 2](https://www.metropolitan.bg/razshiryavane/istoriya-razvitie-metro/razshirenie-etap-2); [stage 3](https://www.metropolitan.bg/razshiryavane/istoriya-razvitie-metro/razshirenie-etap-3); [line 3](https://www.metropolitan.bg/razshiryavane/istoriya-razvitie-metro/liniya-3-metro-sofiya)).

**Sub-scores and why.** *Infrastructure 8:* 33 stations opened and the bus fleet's average age fell from 19.6 to 6.5 years; capital-plan execution in her last years was 65–73% and S&P called it "generally weak". *Waste and environment 5:* the treatment plant opened and the EU court action was dropped, but the plant's fuel was mostly not burned, the incinerator failed in court, and the city lost a class action on air; PM10 compliance was reached at the very end. *Finances 6:* debt grew slowly while revenue nearly doubled and audit opinions were clean, but the heating company's liabilities and the unrecognised transport receivables sat outside the headline debt. *Integrity 4:* no conviction of her team was found and her deputy was acquitted, yet three Audit Office audits found unlawful procurement, unmanaged property and an incompetently supervised transport company, with recommendations to the mayor left unimplemented. *Planning 4:* by her own statement the building boom produced districts "without any infrastructure", and no private plot was expropriated for a park in 13 years. *Services 6:* public-transport satisfaction peaked at 74% (2015) and stray dogs fell by two thirds, while about 10,000 children were unplaced at each first ranking in 2020–2022.

## Terziev (2023– , provisional): candid about failures, short on delivery — 5.2

Vasil Terziev won the 5 November 2023 runoff by **4,786 votes** (175,044 to 170,258; 48.20% to 46.89%) on 34.64% turnout, the narrowest result in the city's recent history; these are secondary-source figures ([en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Sofia_mayoral_election); [Sofia Globe](https://sofiaglobe.com/2023/11/06/bulgarias-2023-mayoral-elections-vassil-terziev-elected-mayor-of-sofia/)). ## What could not be verified

**Election data.** The Central Election Commission's result pages could not be opened. Every vote count, percentage and turnout figure in this report is secondary (press reports of the municipal election commission, or Wikipedia). Nothing was found for the 1995 and 2011 mayoral results; turnout is missing for 1995, 2003, 2005 (official), 2007, 2011 and 2015; small conflicts remain (2015: 60.17% versus 60.13%; 2007: 53.43% versus 53.58%). The 1995 and 1999 seat counts are the researchers' own counts from the council's member lists, and whether the "Radicals" ran a separate list in 1999 is unresolved; the printed 1999 bulletin exists only on paper ([IME library](http://library.ime.bg/byuletin-za-rezultatite-ot-mestnite-izbori-proizvedeni-na-16-i-23-oktomvri-1999g-/)).

**Money before 2010.** No outstanding-debt figure exists in the notes for any year 1996–2009 or for end-2013; the Ministry of Finance register was blocked. Adopted budget totals are missing for 1996–1999, 2002–2003, 2005, 2007–2009, 2011 and 2013–2017. The amount and terms of the Japanese metro loan, whether the EUR 66 m EBRD loan was signed, and municipal privatisation revenue were not found. No S&P history before 2006 or for 2007–2009 was found. The financial ratings for Sofiyanski and Borisov are therefore low-confidence.

**Audits not located or not read.** The 2005 Audit Office reports on the metro and on the water concession are known through the president's interview and one part-report; the Sofia-specific report behind the 19-municipality property summary, and the state financial-control audit of Sofiyski imoti (the "BGN 33 m"), are cited through the press. No Audit Office performance audit of the municipality itself was found for any period after 2014 apart from the air, transport-company, heating-company and district audits, and none for 2024–2026. The 2017 financial-audit opinion is unreadable.

**Court outcomes still open or unknown.** Whether Sofiyanski's acquittals were appealed; the final result of the 2021 air-pollution class action; the final ruling on the 2016 fare ordinance; the developer's main lawsuit over the "Golden Century" permit; the European Public Prosecutor's check and the appeal against the 5% correction on Graf Ignatiev; the Barbutov case after March 2026; the retrial of the 2023 election challenge; the merits of the parking ordinance; the outcome of a contractor's arbitration claim over the 2009 concession termination.

**Council record.** No vote tally was found for the 1999 water concession, for Decision 898 of 2022, or for the plan votes of 2006 and 2009. No count, area or value exists for swaps approved by the council before 2006. No dataset on councillor attendance, roll-call voting or board fees exists for any term. Whether allegations against chair Andrey Ivanov led to proceedings is unknown, as are the date and party of the reported councillor arrest.

**Service data.** Unplaced children by year for 2009–2019; kindergartens built per term from an independent source; fleet purchases by year; fares before 2008; station-level air data for 2014–2021 and any official PM2.5 series; building permits and dwellings completed by year; green space per resident; year-by-year liabilities of Toplofikatsiya for 1995–2023. The topics of Zone B-18, Borisova gradina construction and the Dondukov repair penalties could not be sourced, so nothing is asserted about them.

**Snippet-only items.** Items marked **N** were seen only as search summaries, mainly from one business weekly that blocks automated access; they should be checked by hand before being quoted as exact. They include the kindergarten shortfall quoted for 2008 by a second article, the 2011 dog count, the 2010 draft budget, councillor pay rules of 2012, and the February 2026 competition vote.

**Self-reported claims without independent check.** Fandakova's "130 kindergartens", "42.5 km of metro" and fleet-renewal percentages; Terziev's EUR 220 m waste saving, the 73% cut in the first-group shortfall, the 20-fold fall in untreated landfilling and the "better than Germany" loan terms. No independent poll of Terziev's approval as mayor was found.

## Conclusion

The evidence separates two things that are usually blurred in Sofia's politics. Building is where the city succeeded, and it succeeded most when someone else paid: the 33-station surge of 2012–2021 rode about EUR 805 m of EU grants that Sofiyanski never had and that S&P expects to thin out after the current programme cycle. Control is where every administration failed in the same way. The Audit Office's findings read like one report issued four times: no property policy (1998–2005), no auctions for swaps (2006–2008), unlawful procurement and outdated strategy (2010–2012), recommendations on the transport company ignored (2016–2019). The council's composition changed completely over that span and the finding did not, which suggests the weakness sits in how the council supervises the mayor and the companies, not in which party holds the chair.

Two implications follow for anyone using these ratings. First, the next test is no longer the metro but the liabilities that sit outside the municipal balance sheet: a heating company owing EUR 1.32 bn, a transport system whose fare revenue covers little more than a third of its budget, and a landfill with a few years of life. None of the four mayors resolved these; each passed them on larger. Second, the ratings will shift with data that exists but could not be reached. Official vote counts, the pre-2010 debt register and the unread audits could each move a sub-score, and Terziev's mark depends on whether 345 vehicles, seven stations and a parking reform arrive before autumn 2027 or remain financing announcements.
